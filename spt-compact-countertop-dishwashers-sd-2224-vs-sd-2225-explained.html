Honest take · SPT SD-2224 & SD-2225

SPT's other countertop line. Explained, not guessed.

The SD-9263 family gets all the attention in this category. Behind it sits a smaller, cheaper SPT countertop series — SD-2224 and SD-2225 — that most roundups skip entirely. Here's what the model numbers and suffixes actually tell you, and what they don't.

Say "SPT dishwasher" in this category and most people picture the SD-9263 family: the 18-inch, full-height machine on castors that anchors our own comparison of countertop versus 18-inch portables and shows up in nearly every roundup built around the brand. What that lineup crowds out is a second, smaller SPT series entirely — the SD-2224 and SD-2225 countertop models, three compact listings that sit on a counter, run around $250 to $292, and rarely get a page of their own.

This page is only about that smaller series: what actually changes between an SD-2224 and an SD-2225, what the DS, DWA and DW suffixes tell you without guessing at a decoder SPT never published, and the real price gap between the three listings. If you already know you want the bigger 18-inch class instead, skip ahead to who should buy this line over SPT's 18-inch models.

Why it gets skipped

Why the compact line gets lost behind the 18-inch SD-9263 family

SPT is not a one-model brand, but most buyer guides write as if it is. The SD-9263 family — the freestanding, 18-inch machine on wheels — is the version that competes directly against BLACK+DECKER and Hamilton Beach for the "full-size portable" slot in comparison articles, so that's the SPT listing that gets photographed, specced out and slotted into tables. A roundup built around eight-place-setting machines on castors has no obvious place to put a six-place countertop dial model that costs under $300, so it simply doesn't show up, even though both wear the same SPT badge.

SPT is also not the only brand that does this. Kenmore puts an 18-inch compact and two very different 24-inch hybrid-tub built-ins under one shared name, which we break down in our guide to Kenmore's three 'portable' dishwashers — the pattern there is the same one at work here: one brand name covering two or three genuinely different machines, and most roundups only ever cover the one built for the comparison they're already writing.

The result is a gap specifically for anyone whose kitchen doesn't want or can't fit an 18-inch floor machine but still wants a brand with a long track record in this category. The SD-2224 and SD-2225 models fill that gap — they're just never the ones a "best SPT dishwasher" search surfaces first.

Model numbers

What SD-2224 versus SD-2225 actually changes

Interior material

Of the three listings, only one states its interior material in the title: the SPT SD-2224DS is sold as a "Compact Countertop Dishwasher with Stainless Steel Interior," and it also spells out a 6-place-settings rack with a silverware basket. Neither SD-2225 listing — the SD-2225DWA or the SD-2225DW — states an interior material or a place-setting count in its title. That doesn't mean the tub is plastic; it means the one fact we can confirm from the published listing, SPT chose to lead with for the 2224 and left out for both 2225 variants. If interior material decides your purchase, the SD-2224DS is the only one of the three that tells you upfront, and we'd check the full listing photos and spec sheet on the SD-2225 pair before assuming either way.

Certification

ENERGY STAR is the other line that splits unevenly. The SD-2224DS states ENERGY STAR certification in its title, and so does the SD-2225DWA. The SD-2225DW's title does not make that claim at all — same series, same three-letter model number, no stated certification. So "SD-2224 versus SD-2225" isn't a clean one-for-one swap on certification either: it's closer to two of the three listings stating ENERGY STAR and one not, and the one that doesn't happens to be the model in the middle of the three on price.

Model codes

Reading the DS, DWA and DW suffixes without guessing

SPT does not publish a public legend for how it assigns suffix letters across its countertop line, and that's exactly how roundups end up asserting that "DWA" secretly stands for something it doesn't. The honest approach is to stop trying to decode the letters as a universal key and instead read the one thing that's actually reliable for each listing: the title and stated specs SPT itself wrote for that specific ASIN.

Here's what that gives us, listing by listing, with nothing added beyond what's stated:

  • SD-2224DS — the only one of the three with a stated stainless steel interior and a stated 6-place-settings rack with silverware basket, plus ENERGY STAR. It's also the most expensive of the three at $292.19.
  • SD-2225DWA — states ENERGY STAR certification and delay start with LED, in white, at $250.23, the cheapest of the three.
  • SD-2225DW — states delay start with LED, in white, at $278.14, but its title stops short of the ENERGY STAR claim the DWA listing makes.

In other words: the letters track real differences between these three specific listings, but they're not a decoder ring you can apply to a fourth SPT model you haven't looked up. Treat each suffix as a label for one Amazon listing's stated spec sheet, not as a code with a fixed meaning across the brand — the same caution applies whenever the same base model number shows up more than once, which is also why near-identical listings at different prices are worth a second look; see why the same portable dishwasher shows up at two different prices on Amazon.

What doesn't change

Delay start and LED display: the one feature all three share

Whatever else separates these three listings, delay start and an LED display are common to the SD-2224DS, the SD-2225DWA and the SD-2225DW. That's worth noting precisely because it's the one spec that doesn't split along the model-number or suffix lines the way interior material and certification do — it's a baseline feature of the series rather than a differentiator between the three.

Practically, delay start means you can load the machine and set it to begin a cycle hours later — useful for running it overnight on an off-peak utility rate, or finishing a wash right as you get home, without anyone needing to be in the kitchen when the cycle starts. The LED display is what you use to set that delay and track where the cycle is, which matters more on a compact countertop machine than it sounds: without a window into the tub, a lit display is the only way to tell a running cycle from a finished one at a glance.

Because this feature is constant across all three, it's not a reason to pick one SD-2224/SD-2225 listing over another — the interior material, the certification claim and the price are what actually separate them.

The decision

Who should buy this line instead of SPT's bigger 18-inch models

Buy the SD-2224/SD-2225 line if

Any one of these is true

You have counter space but no floor space to spare for an 18-inch machine on castors. You're washing for one or two people, where six place settings or fewer is realistically enough. Your budget tops out well under $300. Or you simply want a known SPT name without paying for the 18-inch platform's extra capacity you won't use every day.

Buy SPT's 18-inch SD-9263 family instead if

All of these are true

You're regularly washing for three or more people and need the larger capacity a full-height, freestanding machine holds. You have an 18-to-19-inch strip of floor near the sink the machine can live on. And your budget stretches well past $300 toward the 18-inch class's typical range. The jump in capacity between an 18-inch machine and a compact countertop one is larger than the price difference implies — see the capacity jump most buyers miss for the specifics.

Fit matters as much as capacity here. Compact countertop machines like these three still need real clearance under a wall cabinet and a level spot near the sink — the same clearance math that applies to every countertop model in this category, not just SPT's. If you haven't measured yet, work through our cabinet-clearance guide before ordering any of the three listings above, since a tight fit disqualifies a counter spot faster than price does.

FAQ

Common questions about SPT's compact line

What's the actual difference between the SPT SD-2224DS and SD-2225DWA?

Based on what each listing states, the SD-2224DS is the only one with a stated stainless steel interior and a stated 6-place-settings rack with a silverware basket; the SD-2225DWA's title states neither. Both state ENERGY STAR certification and delay start with LED. The SD-2224DS costs $41.96 more.

What do the DS, DWA and DW suffixes on SPT dishwashers mean?

SPT doesn't publish a public legend for these suffixes, so there's no universal decoder to apply. Reading each listing on its own terms: SD-2224DS states a stainless interior and a place-setting count; SD-2225DWA states ENERGY STAR certification; SD-2225DW's title states neither claim. Treat the letters as a label for that specific Amazon listing's stated spec sheet, not a fixed code across the brand.

Are all three SPT SD-2224/SD-2225 models ENERGY STAR certified?

No. The SD-2224DS and the SD-2225DWA both state ENERGY STAR certification in their titles. The SD-2225DW's title does not make that claim, even though it's priced between the other two.

Should I buy this compact line or SPT's 18-inch SD-9263 family?

The compact SD-2224/SD-2225 line fits kitchens with counter space but limited floor space, and suits one or two people washing six or fewer place settings. SPT's 18-inch SD-9263 family holds more and costs roughly double, but needs 18 to 19 inches of floor near the sink. Floor space and household size are the two things that decide it.
