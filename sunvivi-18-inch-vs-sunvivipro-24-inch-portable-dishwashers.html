Comparison · Sunvivi vs SUNVIVIPRO

Same name, different class.

Sunvivi and SUNVIVIPRO sell dishwashers under names close enough to type by mistake, and SUNVIVIPRO alone badges two completely different machines: an 18-inch countertop unit and a 24-inch built-in. Here's what the name doesn't tell you, and what the full model line does.

Search "Sunvivi dishwasher" on Amazon and the results split into two brand names that look like typos of each other: Sunvivi and SUNVIVIPRO. They are not the same listing styled two ways. Sunvivi's name sits on an 18-inch countertop machine. SUNVIVIPRO's name sits on a near-identical 18-inch countertop machine — and, separately, on a 24-inch built-in dishwasher that has nothing in common with either countertop unit except eight letters at the front of its title.

That is three listings and two genuinely different product classes, and the overlap in naming is exactly the kind of thing that gets a buyer halfway through checkout on the wrong machine. This page compares the three on the only things we have in writing: published tank size, plate fit, programs, place settings, certification and price — then tells you which model name to actually read before you click buy.

The name problem

Two brand names, three listings, two classes

Lay the three listings side by side and the pattern is clear. The SUNVIVIPRO 18" Portable Countertop Dishwasher (ASIN B0GL7TB2CP) and the Sunvivi Portable Dishwasher 18'' Countertop (ASIN B0FQV49JFJ) are both 18-inch countertop machines with a 152OZ water tank — so close in spec that the brand suffix is the easiest way to tell their listings apart at a glance. Then there's the SUNVIVIPRO 24" Built-in Dishwasher (ASIN B0FPG1ZLK6), stainless steel, 14–16 place settings — a full built-in unit that shares a brand name with the countertop SUNVIVIPRO listing and nothing else.

In other words: "SUNVIVIPRO" is not a product line, it's a seller name that currently badges two different machine classes, and "Sunvivi" without the PRO suffix is a separate seller name badging a third listing that is, on paper, nearly identical to one of SUNVIVIPRO's. A shopper skimming thumbnails on a search results page has every reason to assume these are variants of one product. They aren't. One pair shares a class; the third does not share anything but a prefix.

This is a pattern we've documented elsewhere in this category too — see how Kenmore's lineup splits into three very different machines under one name, and how EdgeStar's three countertop listings differ despite looking alike. Brand-name overlap in this category is common enough that reading past the brand into the model number and class is the only reliable habit.

The countertop pair

Sunvivi's 18-inch countertop pair, spec for spec

Both countertop listings describe the same physical footprint — 17.7 inches wide by 17.3 inches deep — and the same core feature set: a removable 152-ounce water tank (about 4.5 liters, or 1.2 gallons), dual water supply so the unit fills from the tank by hand or connects directly to a faucet for automatic intake, and six wash programs — Standard, Quick, Intensive, Eco, Fruit and Dry. The Quick program is stated to finish in 29 minutes, and the drying step runs at a high temperature the listings put at 167°F. Both machines are stated to fit plates up to 12 inches, wider than the 10 to 10.25 inches most compact countertop machines in this category cap out at.

Where the two listings actually diverge is in what each one chooses to put in its title. The SUNVIVIPRO-badged unit (B0GL7TB2CP) states a 12-hour delay start and Energy Star certification right in the product name. The Sunvivi-badged unit (B0FQV49JFJ) states neither — its title is shorter and only confirms the 18-inch footprint and the tank. That doesn't prove the second machine lacks a delay timer or a certification; it means the seller didn't put either claim in writing where we can check it, so we won't credit the Sunvivi unit with features only the SUNVIVIPRO listing actually states.

A tank sized in fluid ounces rather than liters is itself a small tell — most of this category's tank-fed countertop machines (the well-water-friendly tank models we've covered separately among them) quote a 5-liter tank, which converts to roughly 169 ounces. At 152 ounces, both Sunvivi-badged machines hold slightly less water per fill than that more common 5-liter spec — a difference worth noting if you were expecting parity with a different brand's tank-fed unit.

What neither countertop listing states

Neither title nor the published bullets give a place-setting count for either 18-inch unit. "Fits up to 12-inch plates" tells you the rack geometry, not how many plates, bowls, glasses and sets of cutlery fit in one load. That's a real gap if you're trying to size this machine against an eight-place-setting rival, and it's one we won't paper over with a guessed number — if the place-setting figure matters to you, treat its absence here as the listing's answer, and check the current listing yourself before ordering.

The built-in

SUNVIVIPRO's 24-inch built-in, and what that cutout requires

The SUNVIVIPRO 24" Built-in Dishwasher (B0FPG1ZLK6) is stated at 14 to 16 place settings, finished in stainless steel, running eight wash programs and Energy Star Certified. That place-setting range alone roughly doubles what a well-specified countertop machine elsewhere in this category claims, and it's the number that matters most here: a place setting is a defined unit — a dinner plate, a side plate or bowl, a glass, a cup and saucer, and one person's cutlery — so 14 to 16 settings is close to or above what a standard full-size kitchen dishwasher is built to hold.

A 24-inch built-in is also a different category of purchase before you ever load a dish. "Built-in" means designed to slide into a base-cabinet opening and stay there: a dedicated 24-inch cutout, a hardwired or plugged electrical connection within reach, a permanent water-supply line, and a drain line routed to the sink's plumbing or a garbage disposal connection — work that, in a rental, generally needs the landlord's sign-off and in any home needs either real DIY plumbing experience or a licensed installer. None of that applies to either countertop unit above, which need nothing but a power outlet and, for faucet mode, a tap with a removable aerator.

That installation gap is the real split this page is about. Two of these three listings are appliances you unbox and plug in. The third is a kitchen renovation component that happens to also be a dishwasher. If you came here assuming "SUNVIVIPRO" meant one machine with options, the built-in is the listing that corrects that assumption fastest — and it's a useful moment to also read what's actually covered on a portable or built-in dishwasher's warranty, since an installed unit is far more expensive to swap than a countertop one if support turns out thin.

Money

Price doesn't track capacity evenly between the two lines

On paper the jump from countertop to built-in looks almost proportionate to price: the SUNVIVIPRO countertop unit runs $259.99, the Sunvivi countertop unit runs $299.99, and the SUNVIVIPRO built-in runs $489.99 — roughly 1.6 to 1.9 times the countertop price. If place settings scaled the same way, that would be a clean trade. They don't, because we can't actually make that comparison: the built-in states 14 to 16 place settings, while neither countertop listing states a place-setting figure at all. The honest version of this math is that we know one side of the ratio and not the other — the built-in may hold substantially more than twice what either countertop unit holds for well under twice the price, or the gap may be smaller than it looks. Without a published figure for the countertop pair, claiming a precise multiplier would be inventing a number the listings don't give us.

The clearer mismatch sits inside the countertop pair itself. The SUNVIVIPRO-badged unit is the cheaper of the two at $259.99, yet its title is the one that states the extra features — the 12-hour delay start and the Energy Star certification. The Sunvivi-badged unit costs $40 more at $299.99 and states fewer claims in writing, on a footprint, tank size and program list that otherwise reads identically. Price here doesn't track published feature count, let alone capacity — it's a reminder that a higher number on a near-identical listing isn't buying you something we can point to in writing.

We've seen a version of this same mismatch before: BLACK+DECKER's $299 countertop and $610 18-inch portable don't scale cleanly either, and SPT's compact SD-2224 and SD-2225 line shows the same pattern at smaller dollar amounts — a model-number or suffix difference moving the price without a matching jump in what's stated on the listing.

The decision

Why checking the full model name matters before you click buy

Everything above reduces to one habit: read past the brand name into the model number and the class word — "countertop" versus "built-in," 18-inch versus 24-inch — before you click buy. "SUNVIVIPRO" on its own tells you the seller, not the machine. The same eight letters sit on a $259.99 appliance you plug in today and a $489.99 appliance that needs a cabinet cutout and a plumber's afternoon. Confusing the two isn't a hypothetical: a search results page, a quick scroll, and a shopper after "the Sunvivi one" can land on either class without noticing the difference until the box arrives the wrong shape.

The same discipline applies across the Sunvivi/SUNVIVIPRO line itself. Both countertop listings read as close to identical twins on tank size, footprint and programs, but only one states a delay-start timer and an Energy Star certification in writing, and it's also the cheaper of the two. Buying on brand recognition alone — "I'll get the Sunvivi one" — means picking between a $40 price difference and a feature claim neither listing lets you verify both ways at once.

Our rule of thumb for this entire category: treat the brand name as a search term, not a specification. The listing title, the class word, and the stated numbers are the only things that tell you what's actually in the box. If you want a second example of exactly this pattern playing out with a different brand, EdgeStar's three countertop listings are worth the five minutes it takes to see how often this happens across the category, not just here.

FAQ

Common questions about Sunvivi and SUNVIVIPRO

Is SUNVIVIPRO the same company as Sunvivi?

They are separate brand names on Amazon, not one brand styled two ways. The names are close enough — one letter and a "PRO" suffix apart — that it's easy to assume they're variants of the same listing. They aren't: each name is attached to its own product listing with its own ASIN, price and published specifications, and SUNVIVIPRO's name additionally appears on a 24-inch built-in dishwasher that has nothing to do with either countertop unit beyond the shared brand name.

What does a "152OZ water tank" actually hold?

152 fluid ounces converts to roughly 4.5 liters, or about 1.2 gallons — one fill's worth of water for one wash cycle on either countertop unit. That's slightly less than the 5-liter (about 169-ounce) tank quoted on several other tank-fed countertop dishwashers in this category, so if you're comparing against a 5-liter machine, expect a marginally smaller fill on these two.

Can the SUNVIVIPRO 24-inch built-in replace a countertop dishwasher without a plumber?

Generally no. A 24-inch built-in dishwasher is designed to slide into a base-cabinet cutout and connect permanently: a dedicated electrical connection, a water-supply line, and a drain line tied into the sink's plumbing or a garbage disposal fitting. That's categorically different work from either countertop unit in this comparison, which need only a power outlet and, in faucet mode, a tap with a removable aerator. Renters in particular should confirm installation is permitted before buying a built-in model.

Why does the cheaper SUNVIVIPRO countertop unit list more features than the pricier Sunvivi one?

We can only go on what each listing states, and the SUNVIVIPRO-badged 18-inch unit's title includes a 12-hour delay start and an Energy Star certification that the Sunvivi-badged unit's title does not mention, despite an otherwise near-identical footprint, tank size and program list. That doesn't prove the Sunvivi unit lacks those features — only that they aren't written into its current listing, which is the only basis we have for comparing the two honestly.
