The pattern

Why you won't find these three in most roundups

Roundup articles are built from whatever is easiest to compare apples-to-apples, and these three listings make that harder than the established shelf does. EUHOMY and COMFEE' each sell several countertop models under one recognizable name, with a consistent run of bullets across the lineup — tank size, place settings, decibel rating, cycle names — that a writer can line up in a table without gaps. HOMCOM, Garvee and Jeaieaiu each show up here as a single listing rather than a documented lineup, and the three bullets each one publishes don't cover the same ground: one states a place-setting count and skips a temperature figure, another states a temperature figure and skips the tank size, and so on. A roundup that needs six comparable columns drops the rows with holes in them, and all three of these have holes.

There's also a plainer reason: unfamiliarity. HOMCOM is a real, multi-category home-goods brand with products well beyond dishwashers, but it isn't a name most US kitchen-appliance shoppers already know the way they know EUHOMY in this specific category. Garvee and Jeaieaiu are less recognizable still, and a listing without reviews, a rating, or a brand history attached to it is a harder sell for a roundup writer to vouch for — which, to be clear, is also exactly why we're not vouching for these three over the established shelf. We're simply reading what each one publishes and comparing it on its own terms.

Capacity and programs

Place settings and programs: what $160 buys versus $240

Program count rises steadily with price across the three: four programs at $159.99, five at $216.59, six at $219.99. That part is the expected pattern — more money buys more wash profiles, which on paper means more flexibility for glassware, baby bottles or a quick cycle. It's the other number, place settings, where the pattern breaks.

Only the $216.59 HOMCOM listing states a place-setting figure at all: 4 place settings, alongside its 6-liter (1.6-gallon) built-in tank. The $159.99 listing and the $219.99 listing publish programs, water source and other features, but neither states how many place settings — or how many pieces — the interior actually holds. That's a real gap, not a rounding error: a place setting is the industry's standard capacity unit precisely so a shopper can compare machines without opening the box, and two of these three listings skip it entirely.

If a listing doesn't give you that number, the honest move is not to guess at one on its behalf. Check the current listing images and the Q&A section for buyer-reported dish counts before assuming either unlisted machine matches HOMCOM's stated four settings — it might hold more, it might hold less, and at this price tier the interior rack layout varies more than the exterior dimensions suggest.

What it's made of

ABS and PP plastic bodies, and what that means after a couple of years

None of these three listings states an interior tub material in its bullets, which is itself typical of this price band — sub-$250 countertop dishwashers in general are built around ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) and PP (polypropylene) plastic housings rather than a stainless-steel tub, because stainless is the costlier part of the bill of materials in a full-size machine and gets cut first when a listing is priced to undercut the shelf above it. This is a general fact about the class these three compete in, not a confirmed spec for any one of the three ASINs — check each listing's images and material callouts before assuming.

What a plastic-bodied tub means in practice, over years rather than weeks: ABS and PP hold up fine to normal dish soap and warm water, but they're more prone than stainless to scratching from abrasive scrubbers, to staining from tomato-based sauces and turmeric, and to gradual clouding from repeated exposure to the 150-plus-degree water these machines run. None of that stops the machine from working. It does mean the interior looks its age faster than a steel tub does, and that a cracked plastic spray arm or a warped rack — the parts that take the most daily stress — is a more common failure point on a budget machine than a pump or heating element giving out. A plastic housing is also lighter, which is a genuine upside for anyone moving the machine between a counter and storage, or into an RV.

Heat

167°F, 158°F, and one listing that won't say: temperature claims compared

Two of the three listings print a specific wash temperature: 167°F for the $159.99 Garvee machine, 158°F for the $216.59 HOMCOM unit. The $219.99 Jeaieaiu listing states six programs but no temperature figure for any of them, which — consistent with its missing place-setting count — is a pattern of this particular listing rather than a one-off omission.

What 158°F to 167°F is actually doing: it's hotter than a hand can tolerate for more than an instant, which is the main reason a dishwasher cleans better than hand-washing for everyday plates and glasses — hot water dissolves grease and loosens food residue faster than lukewarm water, with or without a hard scrub. It is not, on its own, a sanitizing cycle in the commercial-kitchen sense. Commercial sanitizing standards generally call for a sustained final-rinse temperature well above 180°F, held for a specific duration, verified by equipment these countertop machines don't carry. Treat "167°F high-temperature washing" as exactly that — a wash temperature claim describing how hot the water gets, not a certified sanitization result, and a manufacturer-stated figure we have no way to independently confirm.

The 9-degree gap between 158°F and 167°F is unlikely to be the deciding factor between these two machines on its own — both are well into the range where soap and hot water do their job on ordinary dishes. The more useful takeaway is the asymmetry: two listings are willing to print an exact number, one isn't, and that's consistent with the broader disclosure gap running through all three.

FAQ

Common questions about these three listings

Why don't HOMCOM, Garvee and Jeaieaiu show up in most "best dishwasher" roundups?

Mostly because the three listings don't publish the same set of specifications as each other or as the established EUHOMY and COMFEE' lineups, which makes them hard to slot into a standard comparison table. Each one states some figures and omits others — a place-setting count here, a temperature claim there — and unfamiliar single-listing brand names without a review history are a harder recommendation for most roundup writers to stand behind.

Does a 167°F or 158°F wash temperature sanitize dishes?

Not in the certified commercial sense. Both figures are manufacturer-stated wash temperatures, hot enough to cut through grease and food residue far better than hand-washing, but well below the sustained, equipment-verified temperatures that commercial sanitizing standards require. Treat these as wash-performance claims, not sanitization guarantees, and note that the $219.99 Jeaieaiu listing doesn't state a temperature figure at all.

Is the HOMCOM, Garvee or Jeaieaiu dishwasher worth it over an EUHOMY or COMFEE' model?

If you're on a tight budget, furnishing a short-term rental or RV, or washing for one or two people, any of these three can do the job for $30 to $150 less than the established shelf. If you want a listing that states its place-setting count, tank size and decibel rating in one place — the kind of disclosure a documented multi-model lineup usually provides — the established brands are the safer spend.

What's the real difference between a tank-fed and a faucet-fed budget dishwasher?

A tank-fed machine, like the Garvee and HOMCOM listings here, needs only a power outlet — you hand-fill the reservoir before each cycle, which caps how much the machine can spend rinsing. A faucet-fed machine connects to a tap with a removable aerator and refills itself mid-cycle with no manual filling. The Jeaieaiu listing is the only one of the three offering both modes in a single unit.
