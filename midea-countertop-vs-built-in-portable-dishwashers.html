Comparison · One brand, two machines

Midea makes both. They are not the same appliance.

Search "Midea portable dishwasher" and two completely different machines come back under the same name: a tank-fed box the size of a microwave, and a slimline 18-inch cabinet built to sit under a counter on adjustable legs. One brand, one search term, two products that solve opposite problems.

Midea is the rare brand in this category that actually makes more than one kind of machine, rather than relabeling a single Chinese factory platform under five storefront names. Its countertop unit is a sealed tub with a 1.32-gallon built-in tank, a glass door, and a baby-bottle holder — it runs from a power socket and nothing else. Its 18-inch line is a stainless-steel-tub cabinet with adjustable legs, designed to be set into a dishwasher opening the way a built-in model is, not rolled around a kitchen on castors the way some "portable" 18-inch machines in this category are built. Both get sold, searched for and reviewed under the umbrella term "portable dishwasher." They are not interchangeable.

This page exists because that confusion is common and expensive to get wrong. Order the wrong class and you discover it with the box open: the countertop machine sitting on a counter with nowhere to drain, or the built-in-style cabinet arriving to a kitchen with no dishwasher opening to put it in. We compare the three Midea listings in our database spec-by-spec — capacity, installation, price and who each one actually fits — then lay out which class a renter should buy.

Why one name, two machines

Why the same brand covers two very different machines here

Most of the brand names in the portable-dishwasher category sell one product line, sourced from one of a small number of contract manufacturers, under a single storefront identity — which is exactly what makes a brand guide to who actually makes these machines worth reading before you buy on name recognition alone. Midea breaks that pattern: it is a genuine large-scale appliance manufacturer with its own factories, not a storefront reselling someone else's tooling, and the "portable" category is where two of its separate product lines happen to overlap in Amazon's search results.

The first line is the compact countertop tank machine — the Midea Countertop Dishwasher with 1.32Gal Tank, Baby Care, 5+1 Programs (ASIN B0DJVMTSJS). It is a self-contained appliance with its own tank, pump and heater, built to the same footprint as every other brand's countertop box. The second line is the Midea 18 Inch Built-in Dishwasher, 8 Place Settings, Stainless Steel Tub, sold under two listings for finish — white (B0C84KHMSY) and black (B0C84HDJCC). That second machine is not a scaled-up version of the countertop unit; it's Midea's slimline built-in platform, specced with adjustable legs so it can be set into a standard dishwasher opening, sized and marketed toward the "portable" search term because it is still a freestanding unit rather than a hardwired, cabinet-fronted installation.

So "Midea portable dishwasher" is really shorthand for two products built for two different jobs: wash a handful of dishes with no plumbing at all, or replace something closer to a real dishwasher if your kitchen has — or can be given — an opening for one. Confusing them is the single most common mistake a buyer makes with this brand, because the listings share a manufacturer name, a product category and, frankly, similar stock photography.

Capacity

Capacity and place settings: countertop tank versus 18-inch

The countertop model does not quote a place-setting figure in its listing at all, which is typical of small tank-fed machines — see how we read that absence in our guide to price per place setting. What it does quote is a 1.32-gallon (roughly 5-litre) built-in tank, five wash programs — Baby Care, Delicate, Normal, Quick and Fruit — plus an extra-drying enhancement layered on top. The standout feature for capacity purposes is a dedicated baby-bottle holder that stands bottles upside down so they don't tumble during the cycle, which tells you the machine is sized and designed around a specific small load: bottles, pacifiers, a child's plates, not a family's dinner dishes. The glass door lets you see the load mid-cycle, a detail we go into further in our piece on glass-door portable dishwashers.

The 18-inch built-in-style machine quotes 8 place settings — the standard capacity figure for a full-size compact dishwasher — with a stainless steel tub and interior dimensions the listing says will take plates, pots and pans up to 11 inches in height. That is a meaningfully different machine: a place setting is a defined unit of roughly ten items (a dinner plate, a side plate or bowl, a glass, a cup and saucer, one person's cutlery), so 8 settings is close to what a standard built-in dishwasher holds and is not comparable to the countertop unit's bottle-and-bowl capacity in any useful way.

The practical read: if your washing need is "a handful of baby bottles and small dishes between bigger loads," the countertop tank unit's capacity is exactly matched to the job. If it's "replace hand-washing for a household," only the 8-place 18-inch machine gets there — the countertop unit was never built to compete on volume.

Installation

Installation and footprint, side by side

This is where the two machines diverge hardest, and where the word "portable" is doing the most work to paper over a real difference.

The countertop unit: plug in, fill the tank, done

Because the countertop model runs from its own 1.32-gallon tank, it needs nothing but a power socket and a flat section of worktop. There is no faucet adapter to fit, no aerator thread to check, and no dishwasher opening to find — it goes wherever there's room, including a closet shelf, a dorm desk or an RV counter. That is the entire installation process. The trade-off is the one already covered above: a tank this size caps what the machine can wash per cycle, and you refill it by hand before each run rather than connecting a permanent water line.

The 18-inch built-in-style unit: an opening, legs, and a connection

The 18-inch line is a different kind of "portable." Both finishes list a slimline cabinet body at 22.6 inches deep by 17.6 inches wide by 32.4 inches tall, with adjustable legs — a detail that only matters if the plan is to level the unit into a cut dishwasher opening, the way a genuine built-in is installed. That is a materially bigger project than unboxing a countertop machine: it assumes either an existing dishwasher cutout, common if the unit is replacing a dead built-in, or a cabinet modified to create one, plus a water and drain connection at that location. It is not the roll-to-the-sink-and-back freestanding class some other 18-inch "portable" dishwashers use — the adjustable legs and the "built-in" naming point toward a semi-permanent, under-counter placement rather than a unit that travels around the kitchen floor on casters.

In plain terms: the countertop machine is genuinely portable in the sense most readers mean — move it, store it, take it to a new apartment. The 18-inch machines are "portable" mainly in the sense that they are not permanently plumbed and built into cabinetry at the factory; once installed into an opening, in practice they tend to stay there.

Money

The price gap between the classes, and what it buys

The countertop tank unit runs $283.23 in our database. The two 18-inch built-in-style finishes run $448.97 in white and $448.97 in black — a gap of roughly $118 to $168 over the countertop machine, and a $50 gap between the two finishes of the same 18-inch unit. None of these numbers are fixed; Amazon repricing on this category is frequent, which is exactly why we work through cost per place setting as the more durable comparison than the sticker price alone.

What the extra money buys, reading straight from the two listings:

  • 8 place settings instead of none quoted. The single biggest functional gap — the 18-inch machine is sized to replace hand-washing for a household, the countertop unit is not.
  • A stainless steel tub versus whatever interior finish the countertop unit's tub carries (not specified in its listing) — stainless is generally more resistant to staining and odor retention over years of use.
  • Three extra functions — Heated Dry, Hi-Temp and Sanitize — on top of the base six programs, where the countertop unit offers five programs plus one drying enhancement.
  • An Energy Star designation on both 18-inch finishes, a certification the countertop listing does not claim.
  • Larger interior clearance, with the 18-inch tub rated for plates, pots and pans up to 11 inches tall — bigger cookware than a countertop box, built around small dishes and bottles, is designed to take.

What the extra money does not buy is portability in the everyday sense. You cannot unplug a 32.4-inch, adjustable-leg built-in-style unit and move it to a new counter the way you can the tank machine — it is priced and specced for a kitchen that already has, or will get, a proper opening for it.

The decision

Which Midea class actually fits a renter's kitchen

Buy the countertop tank unit if

Any one of these is true

You rent and cannot modify cabinetry, drill a cutout or run a new water line — a landlord conversation nobody wants to have for a dishwasher. Or your washing need is bottles, pacifiers and small dishes rather than full family loads. Or you have no faucet a dishwasher could attach to in the first place, and no dishwasher opening either. Or you move apartments often and need the machine to come with you. Any one of these settles it in the tank unit's favor.

Buy an 18-inch built-in-style finish if

All of these are true

You have an existing dishwasher opening — most often because a built-in dishwasher failed or was never installed — or landlord permission and the skill to create one; three or more people's dishes to wash per day; and roughly $400 to $450 to spend. Miss the opening, and this class is not a weekend project, it's a kitchen-modification project.

For most renters, that framing answers the question before price or finish even enters it: if there is no dishwasher opening and no path to create one, the countertop tank unit is not a compromise, it's the only version of a "Midea portable dishwasher" that actually installs in an afternoon. Renters who do have an opening — typically because a built-in dishwasher died and the landlord hasn't replaced it — get the better outcome from the 18-inch unit, since it's closer to the appliance the kitchen was designed around in the first place. Either way, measure before you order: a countertop tank unit needs flat worktop and a socket; an 18-inch built-in-style unit needs an opening roughly 18 inches wide with the plumbing already run to it.

FAQ

Common questions about Midea's two dishwasher lines

Are Midea's countertop and 18-inch dishwashers actually the same machine in different sizes?

No. They're built on different platforms for different installation methods. The countertop model is a sealed tub with its own 1.32-gallon tank that needs only a power socket. The 18-inch line is a stainless-tub cabinet with adjustable legs, specced to be levelled into a dishwasher opening — closer to a built-in dishwasher's installation method than to a true plug-and-go portable.

Do the white and black 18-inch Midea dishwashers have different specifications?

Based on both listings, no — both list 8 place settings, a stainless steel tub, the same 22.6 × 17.6 × 32.4-inch body with adjustable legs, the same six programs plus three functions, and the same Energy Star designation. The stated difference is exterior finish and price; in our database the black version runs roughly $50 above the white one.

Can the Midea 18-inch dishwasher be used without a dishwasher cutout?

Its adjustable legs and "built-in" design point toward installation in a dishwasher opening with the water and drain connections already run to that spot, rather than being rolled up to a sink on casters like some other 18-inch "portable" dishwashers. If your kitchen has no such opening, creating one is a real modification project, not a weekend unboxing.

What can the Midea countertop dishwasher actually wash, if it holds no stated place settings?

Its listing is built around a Baby Care program with a bottle holder, plus Delicate, Normal, Quick and Fruit cycles, which points to a bottle-and-small-dish machine rather than a stand-in for a family's full dinner load. Pair it with its 1.32-gallon tank size, and treat it as a tool for keeping up with small daily washing, not a replacement for an 8-place dishwasher.
