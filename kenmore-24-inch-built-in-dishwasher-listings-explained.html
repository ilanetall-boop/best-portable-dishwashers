Three Amazon listings carry Kenmore's 24-inch built-in dishwasher, all in fingerprint-resistant stainless steel, all stating 15 place settings and ENERGY STAR Certified. Two of them — priced at $849.99 and $899.99 — share an identical title: 45 dBA, EasyFlex 3rd Rack, UltraWash Plus, TurboDry and SmartWash. Nothing in either listing's stated specification explains the $50 gap between them. The third listing, also $849.99, reads differently: 49 dBA — four decibels louder — TurboDry, SmartWash and Leak Detection, with no EasyFlex 3rd Rack and no UltraWash Plus named anywhere in the title.

That's an easy trap for a shopper comparing three "Kenmore 24-inch built-in" results on price alone. Buy the $899.99 listing expecting an upgrade over the $849.99 one with the same name, and you'd be paying $50 for a listing the title itself says is identical. Buy either 45 dBA listing assuming it has Leak Detection because "they're all Kenmore 24-inch built-ins," and you'd be wrong about a feature that genuinely isn't stated on that listing. This page works through what each of the three titles actually commits to, where two are provably the same machine at two prices, and what the quieter, third-rack listing trades away for the louder one's safety feature.

The catalog

Why one Kenmore 24-inch model shows up as three separate listings

A single physical dishwasher design doesn't automatically map to a single Amazon product page. In practice, each color, price test or regional batch of the same chassis frequently gets its own ASIN, its own photos and its own price history — and, in a category where Amazon's API returns no rating data at all, its own blank slate with no reviews to distinguish it from its siblings. That pattern shows up across this site: EdgeStar's countertop dishwasher splits into three listings, and SPT's SD-6513 family splits into three more, each along a different line than the one here.

Kenmore's 24-inch built-in splits two ways at once, and they're not the same kind of split. The $849.99 and $899.99 listings are a price split with no stated spec behind it: identical titles — 45 dBA, EasyFlex 3rd Rack, UltraWash Plus, TurboDry, SmartWash, 15 place settings, ENERGY STAR Certified — at two different prices, the same pattern we found with two of Kenmore's own 24-inch Hybrid Tub listings pricing an identical black-and-white pair a few dollars apart. The third listing, also $849.99, is a genuine configuration split: a different noise rating, a different feature list, and a stated spec field — exterior dimensions, 24.61 inches deep by 23.55 wide by 33.88 tall — that neither of the other two listings states at all.

Whether the duplicate pricing on the 45 dBA pair reflects two separate production batches, a stale price that never got reconciled, or simply two ASINs Kenmore's retail partner never merged, isn't something the listing titles settle, and we don't invent an explanation the data doesn't support. What the titles do settle is which listing is which — and that's worth lining up before price becomes the only thing you're comparing.

Noise

49 dBA versus 45 dBA: the real difference between two otherwise similar units

Set the Leak Detection listing's 49 dBA rating next to the other two listings' 45 dBA, and four decibels sounds small until you remember how the scale works: every 10 dB is roughly a doubling of perceived loudness, so a 4 dB gap is a real, audible difference, not rounding noise. For a built-in dishwasher that runs inside a cabinet a few feet from a kitchen table or an open-plan living room, that's the kind of gap a household notices every evening the machine runs during dinner or after the kids are in bed.

Both figures are manufacturer claims rather than independently audited measurements, taken in conditions the listings don't disclose, so treat the 4 dB gap as a strong signal about which machine is built to run quieter — not a guarantee of exactly how either sounds in your kitchen, on your floor, against your cabinets. What the figure does reliably tell you is which of the three listings to start with if noise is the deciding factor: the two $849.99-and-$899.99 listings sharing the 45 dBA rating, not the Leak Detection listing at 49 dBA.

It's also the first clue that the Leak Detection listing isn't simply "the same machine plus a sensor." A sensor and its control-board logic add a real line item to a bill of materials, and somewhere in that same redesign the motor or pump assembly picked up four extra decibels rather than staying identical to the quieter pair. Kenmore's own 24-inch Hybrid Tub line shows the same noise-versus-feature trade in reverse: there, the 54 dBA Hybrid Tub carries more capacity and a lower price than the quieter 45 dBA compact, so a brand's own catalog can price either direction depending on which spec it's selling.

The feature swap

EasyFlex 3rd Rack and Leak Detection: which listing actually has which

This is the part a shopper comparing three "Kenmore 24-inch built-in" results is most likely to get backwards, because the two features read like they could both belong to a flagship listing — and instead they're split cleanly across the two configurations, never both on the same title.

  • EasyFlex 3rd Rack. Named in the $849.99 and $899.99 listings' titles, and absent from the Leak Detection listing's title entirely. A third, dedicated upper tier for flatware, small bowls and odd-shaped items is a feature full-size premium dishwashers popularized and compact or mid-tier built-ins rarely include at all — it's the single clearest reason to prefer the 45 dBA pair if rack flexibility matters more to your loading habits than anything else on this page.
  • UltraWash Plus. Also named only in the 45 dBA pair's titles, sitting above whatever plain "UltraWash" would describe on a lower-tier listing. Neither title breaks down what "Plus" changes mechanically, so we won't guess beyond the name — we can say it's a stated spec on two of the three listings and not stated on the third.
  • Leak Detection. Named only in the 49 dBA listing's title, and absent from both 45 dBA titles. We cover the mechanism in detail in our separate guide to leak detection on portable dishwashers: a float switch or electrical contact pair sits in the base pan under the tub, and if water reaches it, the control board halts the cycle or runs the drain pump until the pan is dry again — a real last line of defense for a built-in sitting inside a fitted cabinet over a finished floor, which is exactly this product class.

Put plainly: the EasyFlex 3rd Rack and UltraWash Plus pair never appears on the same listing as Leak Detection, and Leak Detection never appears on a listing that also states the third rack or the upgraded wash tier. Nothing in the data suggests a fourth, do-everything listing exists. If you want both the rack and the sensor on this specific product, that listing isn't in Kenmore's current Amazon catalog — you're choosing one trade or the other.

What's shared

UltraWash Plus and SmartWash: the sensor technology behind the wash cycle

SmartWash is the one wash-system name that appears in all three titles, and it's worth being precise about what that word is generally understood to describe on a built-in dishwasher before assuming more than the listing states: a turbidity or soil-level sensor that reads how dirty the wash water is partway through the cycle and adjusts wash time, water use or temperature in response, rather than running a single fixed program regardless of how soiled the load actually is. That's a real, useful piece of engineering shared by all three listings here — the one point where the quieter pair and the Leak Detection listing are on genuinely equal footing.

UltraWash Plus is the term that doesn't carry across all three. It's named only in the two 45 dBA listings' titles, sitting above whatever a plain "UltraWash" tier would be on a different product; the Leak Detection listing's title names TurboDry and SmartWash but not UltraWash Plus, so we treat that listing as not stating the upgraded wash tier rather than assuming it's included under a different name. TurboDry, by contrast, is named on all three titles — a drying step distinct from a heating element, using fan-assisted airflow or a timed vent to clear residual moisture once the wash itself finishes.

The practical read: TurboDry and SmartWash are the shared baseline across every listing on this page, so whichever of the three you order, you're getting both. UltraWash Plus is the one wash-system name that's exclusive to the 45 dBA, EasyFlex-rack pair — one more item on the list of what the Leak Detection listing is actually trading away for its sensor and its 49 dBA motor, not a feature you can assume onto a listing whose title doesn't state it.

The $50 question

The picks, and what $50 buys on the most expensive of the three

Set the $849.99 and $899.99 listings' titles side by side and there is nothing left to compare: 45 dBA, EasyFlex 3rd Rack, UltraWash Plus, TurboDry, SmartWash, 15 place settings, ENERGY STAR Certified — every word matches on both. Neither title states a dimension, a different cycle count, or any feature the other doesn't also claim. That's the strongest signal the listing data can give that these are the same appliance under two ASINs, not two different machines that happen to share a title.

Which means, on the data available, the honest answer to "what does the extra $50 buy" is nothing the title documents. We've seen this exact pattern elsewhere in this category — two BLACK+DECKER countertop twins price 76 cents apart for a real spec difference, and two Kenmore Hybrid Tub colors price within a few dollars of each other for no stated difference at all beyond a door color. Here the gap is far larger — $50, not 76 cents or a few dollars — and the title still can't justify it. Separate ASINs price independently on Amazon and routinely drift apart even when the underlying product hasn't changed, which is the simplest explanation for why one of these listings costs more without claiming to be a better machine.

So the straightforward advice for this pair is: buy whichever of the two is cheaper at the moment you're ordering, and don't assume the pricier one is "the better Kenmore" because it costs more. If the $849.99 listing goes out of stock or the gap narrows or reverses, the same logic applies in whichever direction the prices move — read the title, not the price tag, as the source of truth for what you're getting.

Before you click buy

How to tell which listing you're actually ordering before you buy

All three listings share enough — the same brand, the same fingerprint-resistant stainless finish, the same 24-inch built-in format, the same 15 place settings and ENERGY STAR Certified status — that a shopper skimming thumbnails could easily treat them as interchangeable. The title and the stated fields will tell you apart if you check them in this order:

  • Decibel rating first. 45 dBA identifies the EasyFlex 3rd Rack / UltraWash Plus configuration, in either of its two prices. 49 dBA identifies the Leak Detection listing. If a listing states neither number clearly, don't assume which configuration it is from the price alone — the two 45 dBA listings already prove price isn't a reliable signal here.
  • EasyFlex 3rd Rack or Leak Detection, named explicitly. These two features never appear on the same title. Whichever one the listing states is the configuration you're getting; the other is absent, not just unmentioned.
  • Stated exterior dimensions. Only the Leak Detection listing's fields state exact dimensions — 24.61 inches deep, 23.55 wide, 33.88 tall. Neither 45 dBA listing states a dimension at all in the fields we track. If exact clearance matters for your cabinet opening, the Leak Detection listing's fields hand you a number to measure against; on the other two, confirm the dimensions on the live listing before you order rather than assuming a standard 24-inch built-in envelope.
  • Price as the last check, never the first. Two listings here prove that a higher price doesn't reliably mean a better-specified machine. Compare titles before price decides anything.

FAQ

Common questions about Kenmore's 24-inch built-in listings

Are the two $849.99 and $899.99 Kenmore 24-inch listings the same dishwasher?

On the data available, yes. Both titles state the identical specification: 45 dBA, EasyFlex 3rd Rack, UltraWash Plus, TurboDry, SmartWash, 15 place settings and ENERGY STAR Certified. Neither listing claims a feature or dimension the other doesn't also claim, so the $50 gap between them doesn't map to anything the titles document. Buy whichever is cheaper when you order.

What's the actual difference between the 45 dBA and 49 dBA Kenmore 24-inch built-ins?

The 45 dBA listings (at $849.99 and $899.99) name EasyFlex 3rd Rack and UltraWash Plus and don't claim Leak Detection. The 49 dBA listing claims Leak Detection and doesn't name EasyFlex 3rd Rack or UltraWash Plus. All three run TurboDry and SmartWash and hold 15 place settings. Four decibels is a real, audible gap — every 10 dB is roughly a doubling of perceived loudness — and the feature swap is a genuine trade, not an upgrade in either direction.

Does any Kenmore 24-inch built-in have both the EasyFlex 3rd Rack and Leak Detection?

Not among the three listings in our database. EasyFlex 3rd Rack and UltraWash Plus appear only on the two 45 dBA listings; Leak Detection appears only on the 49 dBA listing. We don't find a fourth listing that states both, and we don't assume one listing has a feature the title doesn't claim simply because a similarly branded listing does.

How do I tell which Kenmore 24-inch listing I'm about to order?

Check the decibel rating first — 45 dBA identifies the EasyFlex 3rd Rack / UltraWash Plus configuration, 49 dBA identifies the Leak Detection configuration. Then check which of the two named features (EasyFlex 3rd Rack or Leak Detection) the title states; they never appear together. Only the 49 dBA listing states exact exterior dimensions in the fields we track. Check price last — two of these three listings prove a higher price doesn't reliably mean a better-specified machine.
