Buying Guide · Small Apartments

Small apartment, real dishwasher. Here's what fits.

Counter space is the real constraint, not price. Every machine below is measured against a small kitchen's actual limits — a fixed run of worktop, a faucet that may or may not take an adapter, and wall cabinets that hang about 18 inches up — so you can pick one that fits before it arrives, not after.

A small apartment kitchen has one fact that doesn't move: a fixed run of counter, usually between a stove and a sink, with wall cabinets hanging roughly 18 inches above it. Everything else about buying a portable dishwasher — price, cycles, place settings — is negotiable. That gap isn't. Get the width, the depth with the door open, or the cabinet clearance wrong, and the box goes back to Amazon.

This guide starts from that constraint instead of a generic "best of" list. It covers how to measure before you order, countertop versus 18-inch freestanding for an apartment, six machines ordered by footprint, what a "6 place settings" claim actually holds once real dishes go in, why the decibel rating matters more when the dishwasher runs in the room you live in, and a verdict by kitchen size and household.

The two classes

Countertop or 18-inch freestanding — which suits an apartment

Every portable dishwasher belongs to one of two classes, and for an apartment the choice usually resolves fast. A countertop machine is a sealed tub, roughly 16 to 22 inches wide, that sits on the worktop — and on several machines below, runs from its own water tank with no connection to the faucet at all. An 18-inch freestanding unit like the BLACK+DECKER BPD8B is a genuine dishwasher on wheels: it stands on the floor, rolls to the sink, and needs about 18 inches of open floor plus door swing plus a clear path to get there.

In most small apartments, floor space is scarcer than counter space, which tips the decision toward countertop — a galley kitchen with two feet of clear floor doesn't have 18 to spare permanently, since that strip is also where you stand to cook. A countertop machine occupies worktop instead, a real cost but a more familiar one to negotiate around.

The freestanding class earns its keep when an apartment has the opposite problem: little usable counter, but a spare corner of floor near the sink. The BLACK+DECKER BPD8B — 18 inches wide, ENERGY STAR certified — gives back a usable surface on top of the unit, close to a wash for a kitchen with no counter to lose in the first place. It runs from the faucet only, so it needs a straight tap with a removable aerator — see how to hook up a portable dishwasher, faucet or tank, step by step.

The four smallest machines below — EUHOMY, COMFEE' Minilite, NOVETE and AIRMSEN — sidestep the floor-versus-counter trade-off almost entirely, because "No Hookup Needed" means the machine goes wherever there's an outlet, not wherever there's a tap or a clear floor. That's the biggest advantage a small apartment gets from this category, and why four of the six picks below are tank machines. Capacity

Place settings vs. reality: what fits in a 6-setting basket

A place setting is a defined unit, not marketing language: one dinner plate, one side plate or bowl, one glass, one cup and saucer, and one person's cutlery — roughly ten items. It's how dishwasher capacity has been quoted for decades, which is why "6 place settings" on the COMFEE' model above (B096RYWSQM) is directly comparable to six-place machines elsewhere, even though none of the other five machines here state a place-setting figure at all.

Six settings sounds like a full family dinner, and on paper it nearly is. Two things shrink that number in practice: the count is built on standardized, stackable crockery rather than the mismatched plates and oversized mugs most kitchens actually have, so expect real capacity to land under the claim once a saucepan goes in; and plate diameter is a separate spec — a 6-place claim says nothing about whether an 11-inch plate fits, only that six of whatever size the manufacturer tested will.

The four tank-based machines here — EUHOMY, COMFEE' Minilite, NOVETE and AIRMSEN — don't publish a place-setting number at all, which is worth reading as information: a five-litre tank caps how much water a machine can spend rinsing, and that ceiling tends to show up as a smaller basket rather than a number worth comparing against an eight-place built-in. If capacity is your main constraint, the 6-place COMFEE' is the only machine here you can plan a dinner around with a published number.

The honest takeaway: don't buy on capacity you have to infer. A stated place-setting count is real and comparable; without one, assume the machine is sized for one or two people's daily dishes rather than a dinner party.

Noise

Noise: why 40-45 dB matters when the kitchen is the living room

In a house, a dishwasher runs behind a closed kitchen door and nobody thinks about it. In a studio or one-bedroom apartment, the kitchen is very often the same room as the couch and the bed, so a long cycle running six feet from where you work or sleep is a different proposition. That's why the decibel rating — a spec most roundups treat as a footnote — belongs near the top of the list here.

Only one machine here states a decibel figure: the EUHOMY, rated at 40 dB. Countertop dishwashers in this category are commonly marketed in the 45 to 52 dB range, and every 10 dB is roughly a doubling of perceived loudness, so a 40 dB machine next to a 50 dB one is closer to half as loud to your ear. That 40-to-45 dB band is worth watching if the dishwasher and the sofa share a room: below it you're close to a quiet refrigerator hum, above it you're into territory that competes with quiet conversation.

Two caveats. Decibel figures are measured in undisclosed conditions and aren't independently audited, so 40 dB is a strong signal, not a promise — hard surfaces and a countertop machine, which transmits vibration more than a floor-standing unit on rubber feet, can make a rated figure feel louder in practice. And none of the other five machines here publish a decibel number, which doesn't mean they're loud — it means there's nothing to compare against the EUHOMY's figure, and we won't invent one.

The decision

Verdict, by kitchen size and household

Studio or one galley wall

One of the tank machines, no exceptions

If your entire kitchen is one wall or a kitchenette, the counter and the faucet are both already spoken for. Start with the COMFEE' Minilite or the NOVETE — both run from a 5-litre tank with no hookup required — and measure the width available first. The EUHOMY is the pick if noise matters more than an inch or two of counter, since it's the only one here with a stated 40 dB rating.

One-bedroom, real counter, no floor to spare

The 6-place COMFEE', if the width fits

A household of two or three with 22 inches or more of counter can support the 6-place COMFEE' and its published dimensions: 19.7 in deep, 21.6 in wide, 17.2 in tall. It's the only machine here you can plan real capacity around, but it isn't a fallback if your counter turns out shorter than you thought; in that case, drop back to the AIRMSEN or NOVETE instead.

Two-bedroom or shared apartment, floor space near the sink

Once you're cooking for three or more people and the kitchen has an actual corner of open floor — not counter, floor — the BLACK+DECKER 18-inch on wheels earns its price. It's the only machine here with real capacity for a household rather than one or two people's daily dishes, and it gives back counter space instead of taking more. It only makes sense with both a straight faucet and the floor to park it on; miss either and you're back to the countertop class regardless of household size.

Don't buy for the household you'll have in two years — a machine that fits this apartment moves easily if it's a tank model, less easily if it's an 18-inch unit on wheels. FAQ

Common questions about small-apartment dishwashers

How much clearance do I need above the counter for a portable dishwasher?

Standard American wall cabinets hang roughly 18 inches above the counter — the number to measure against first. The only machine here with a published height is the COMFEE' 6-place model at 17.2 inches, a narrow margin once you account for the door or lid opening. For the tank-based machines, the listings don't state a height, so measure your own cabinet gap before ordering.

Do I need to connect a countertop dishwasher to my kitchen faucet?

Not necessarily. Four of the six machines here — EUHOMY, COMFEE' Minilite, NOVETE and AIRMSEN — are built around a water tank and marketed as needing no hookup at all; you fill them by hand and plug them into an outlet. NOVETE also supports an inlet hose if you'd rather connect to the tap. Only the 18-inch BLACK+DECKER requires a faucet connection, since it has no tank option.

How many place settings do I actually get from a "6 place settings" dishwasher?

A place setting is a defined unit of roughly ten items — a dinner plate, a side plate or bowl, a glass, a cup and saucer, and one person's cutlery. Six settings is close to a full family dinner on paper, but the count is built on standardized crockery rather than your mismatched plates and bowls, so expect real capacity to land a little under the claimed figure, and check plate diameter separately if your plates run larger than average.

Is a countertop or an 18-inch portable dishwasher better for a small apartment?

For most small apartments, countertop wins, because floor space tends to be scarcer than counter space, and several countertop models here run from a tank with no faucet connection needed at all. An 18-inch freestanding machine like the BLACK+DECKER BPD8B makes more sense if your apartment has the opposite shape — little spare counter but an open corner of floor near the sink — since it stands on the floor and gives back a usable surface on top.
