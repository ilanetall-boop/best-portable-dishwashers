EdgeStar has three countertop listings. Here's what actually differs.

Search "EdgeStar countertop dishwasher" on Amazon and three listings come back: a white DWP63 at $329, a black DWP63 at $349, and a third listing in Silver that never uses the DWP63 code at all — and whose own bullets claim a leakage sensor and child lock neither DWP63 title mentions. EdgeStar's countertop dishwasher shows up on Amazon as three separate product pages, and the listings don't make the relationship between them obvious. Two share a model code — DWP63 — and differ only in color and a $20 price gap: white at $329.00, black at $349.00. The third listing drops the DWP63 code from its title entirely and leads instead with a feature list: 6 Place Settings, 7 Wash Cycles, Energy Star Rated, Leakage Sensor, Child Lock, finished in Silver, priced at $336.00. All three state the same 21-5/8 inch width.

That's an unusual spread. Buy the black DWP63 expecting more dishwasher for your extra $20 over white, and the title tells you the only thing you're paying for is the color. Buy the Silver listing thinking it's "just the other EdgeStar one," and you'd miss that it's the only one of the three whose title claims a leakage sensor and a child lock at all — for $13 less than the black DWP63. This page works through what each listing actually states, where the DWP63 pair genuinely is identical apart from color, and which of the three is the better buy depending on what you need from a countertop machine.

The catalog

Why EdgeStar's countertop line shows up as three separate listings

A single Amazon listing is tied to one ASIN, and in an ideal catalog each physical color or finish of a product would at least sit on the same page as a selectable variant. Appliances routinely don't work that way in practice: a white unit, a black unit and a differently-featured unit from the same brand frequently get published as three unrelated product pages instead, each with its own photos, its own price history and its own reviews (or, in this category, no reviews at all, since Amazon's API returns no rating data for these machines).

For EdgeStar's countertop dishwasher, that split happened along two different lines at once. The white and black DWP63 listings are a straightforward color split — same model code, same stated width, same place-setting count, two separate ASINs. The third listing is a different kind of split: rather than being titled as a third DWP63 color, its title is built entirely around feature claims (the cycle count, the certification, the safety features) and never states the DWP63 code. Whether that reflects a genuinely different internal model number, a rebadged version of the same chassis, or simply a listing written by a different template, isn't something the title or our product data settles — and we don't assert an answer the data doesn't support.

What is clear, and what matters more to a buyer than the backstory, is that the three listings are not interchangeable on paper. Two are provably the same machine in two colors. One makes claims the other two don't make at all. EdgeStar isn't unique here — SPT's own compact countertop line splits the same way, with near-identical dial-controlled listings that differ mainly in a suffix letter and a delay-start claim buried in the title rather than the headline spec.

The DWP63 pair

DWP63 black versus white: same machine, different price, no functional gap

Set the white and black DWP63 titles side by side and the pattern is obvious: "EdgeStar DWP63 21-5/8 Inch Wide 6 Place Setting Countertop Dishwasher - White" and "EdgeStar DWP63 21-5/8 Inch Wide 6 Place Setting Countertop Dishwasher - Black." Every word matches except the trailing color. Same model code, same stated width, same place-setting count, no other claim added or dropped on either side. That's the strongest signal available from listing data alone that these two are the identical appliance in two finishes, not two different machines that happen to share a model number.

The price doesn't track any stated difference, either. White lists at $329.00 and black at $349.00 — a $20 gap, roughly 6 percent — for a difference the title itself identifies as cosmetic. Neither title claims a different cycle count, a different capacity, a leakage sensor, or a child lock; neither one states a spec the other doesn't. If you're choosing between the two DWP63 listings purely on function, there's nothing in the published data to choose on. The decision is entirely which color fits your kitchen, and whether $20 for black is worth it to you.

That's worth saying plainly because it's an easy trap in this category: a higher price on an otherwise-identical listing reads as "the better one" by default, the way BLACK+DECKER's two countertop twins price within 76 cents of each other for a real spec difference. Here, EdgeStar's $20 gap buys color and nothing the title states beyond it — which makes the white DWP63 the better deal unless black is a genuine requirement for your kitchen.

The third listing

The listing with a leakage sensor and child lock the DWP63 doesn't have

The third EdgeStar countertop listing reads nothing like the two DWP63 pages. Its full title: "EdgeStar Countertop Dishwasher, 6 Place Settings, 7 Wash Cycles | Energy Star Rated, 21-5/8 Inch Wide, Leakage Sensor, Child Lock, Portable Dishwasher for Apartments and RVs, Silver." No DWP63 code anywhere in it. In its place: a stated cycle count (7, where neither DWP63 title states a number at all), an Energy Star claim (which neither DWP63 title makes), and two features the DWP63 pair's titles never mention — a leakage sensor and a child lock.

  • Leakage sensor. On countertop dishwashers, a stated leakage sensor is generally understood to detect water escaping the sealed tub and stop or alert during the cycle rather than let it run onto the counter unattended — a real safety-relevant spec for a machine that's often left running while you're in another room. Neither DWP63 title claims this feature, so we don't assume either color has it.
  • Child lock. A control-panel lockout that prevents a cycle from being started, changed, or stopped by someone other than the person who set it — useful with kids in the kitchen, and arguably more useful in the RV use case the listing itself calls out, where a bumpy drive can bump buttons a stationary countertop machine never would.
  • 7 wash cycles and Energy Star Rated. Both stated outright in the title, where the DWP63 listings are silent on cycle count and certification entirely. We don't claim the DWP63 pair lacks these — we simply don't have a title or spec field that states them, so we don't assert them either way.

Same 21-5/8 inch width, same 6 place settings as both DWP63 colors — so capacity and footprint aren't the differentiator here. What separates this listing is the feature set its own title is built around, at $336.00: $7 more than the white DWP63, and $13 less than the black one. This is close to the pattern we found working through Kenmore's own three-listing "portable" lineup — a single brand name covering genuinely different feature sets, where the model code (or its absence) tells you more than the brand name does.

Before you click buy

Reading past the shared product photos to confirm which model you're ordering

All three listings sit in the same visual family — a compact countertop chassis with a round control dial, photographed from similar angles, which is exactly what you'd expect whether these are three finishes of one design or three separately listed products from the same manufacturer's catalog. The photos alone won't tell you which listing you're on. The title and the stated fields will, if you check them in order:

  • Model code, or its absence. "DWP63" appears in both the white and black titles and nowhere in the Silver listing's title. That absence is itself informative — it's the single fastest way to tell the Silver listing apart from the color pair at a glance.
  • Stated width. All three state 21-5/8 inches. If a listing you land on states a different width, it isn't one of these three, whatever the photos look like — and the width matters for fit before anything else, since cabinet clearance is usually the first thing that disqualifies a countertop machine, not price.
  • Feature bullets that contradict rather than overlap. A leakage sensor, a child lock, and a 7-cycle count are stated on one listing and absent from the other two's titles. That's not three listings describing the same thing three different ways — it's one listing making claims the other two simply don't make.
  • Price as a tiebreaker, never as the first check. $329, $336 and $349 look close enough to treat as rounding noise, but the gaps map to real differences in what each title states — color for the DWP63 pair, a documented feature set for the Silver listing. Check the title before the price decides anything for you.

If you're ordering by ASIN rather than by clicking through from a search result, double-check it against the table above before you add to cart — on a shared chassis design like this one, the product photo your browser shows first is the least reliable signal on the page.

FAQ

Common questions about EdgeStar's countertop listings

What's actually different between the black and white EdgeStar DWP63?

Based on the published titles, nothing beyond the color and a $20 price gap. Both state the model code DWP63, the same 21-5/8 inch width, and the same 6 place settings. Neither title claims a leakage sensor, a stated cycle count, or any other feature the other doesn't also state — so on the data available, color is the only functional choice between them.

Does the EdgeStar DWP63 have a leakage sensor or child lock?

Neither the white nor the black DWP63 title states a leakage sensor or a child lock. A separate EdgeStar countertop listing, finished in Silver and priced at $336.00, does claim both features in its title, along with a stated 7 wash cycles and an Energy Star rating. We don't assume the DWP63 has these features simply because a similarly branded listing does — if they matter to you, the Silver listing is the one whose title commits to them.

Is EdgeStar's Silver countertop listing a different dishwasher than the DWP63?

It's a separate listing with its own ASIN, and its title never uses the DWP63 model code the white and black listings both use — so we can't confirm from the published data whether it shares the DWP63's internal chassis or is a genuinely different model. What we can confirm is that it states the same 21-5/8 inch width and 6 place settings as both DWP63 colors, plus features (leakage sensor, child lock, 7 wash cycles, Energy Star) that neither DWP63 title claims.

Which EdgeStar countertop listing is the best deal?

It depends on what you need. If color is your only requirement, the white DWP63 at $329.00 is the cheapest and states the same specification as the black DWP63 at $349.00. If a leakage sensor, child lock, stated cycle count or Energy Star rating matter to you, the Silver listing at $336.00 states all four for less than the black DWP63 and only $7 more than white — making it the strongest claim-for-price of the three.
