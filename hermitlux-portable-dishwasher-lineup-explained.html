Honest take · Hermitlux countertop lineup

One brand, three listings. Not one winner.

Hermitlux sells three countertop dishwashers that look, at a glance, like the same box photographed three times. They aren't. Each one answers a different question a shopper asks before anything else — can I see inside it, will it fit my counter, and does the number on the efficiency claim actually mean anything — and no single listing answers all three.

Search "Hermitlux dishwasher" on Amazon and three listings come back, all countertop, all built around a 5-liter water tank, all priced within $48 of each other. That closeness is the trap. It makes the three look like minor variations of one product, when the listings themselves describe three different machines solving three different problems: one leans on a glass door as its defining feature, one is built narrow enough for a kitchen that can't fit a standard countertop box, and one trades the viewing window for a color screen and the most washing programs of the three.

None of that shows up if you sort by price and buy whichever is cheapest that week. This page goes listing by listing — what each one states, what it leaves out, and which problem it was actually built to solve — so the $47.49 gap between the cheapest and the most expensive buys you something specific, or it doesn't.

How this page is built

We compare the three Hermitlux listings on what their product titles and pages actually state — place settings, washing-program counts, built-in features, price — and on general facts about how countertop tank-fed dishwashers work. We do not physically test these machines and we do not run a testing lab, and we publish no star ratings: Amazon's listing data for these three ASINs carries no verified rating or review count, so inventing one, or repeating review sentiment we have no data for, would be the same offense as making up a score.

The setup

Three listings, one brand, three different problems

Line the three titles up and the split is plainer than the product photos suggest. The original listing, the Hermitlux Countertop Portable Dishwasher with Built-in 5-Liter Water Tank (ASIN B08SBR12S7, $284.99), is sold with a Glass Door as its stated variant — the one spec the listing leads with is the window, not a program count or a place-setting figure. The color-screen model (ASIN B0GVXQGFRH, $299.99) states five place settings, eight washing programs and a color screen display outright in its title, and markets itself for apartments and RVs with "no hookup needed." The Slim model (ASIN B0FKY589PL, $332.48) states six washing programs, a glass door and an LED light, and is sold under a Narrow-Space Design variant built around a 14.6-inch-wide body for small counters.

That is not one product with three price points. It's a viewing window, a program count, and a footprint, each sold as the headline feature of a separate SKU. A shopper who wants "the best Hermitlux" is asking the wrong question — the honest version is "which of these three problems do I actually have," because the listings were built to answer different ones.

The price spread backs this up in an unflattering way for the most expensive listing: the Slim model costs $47.49 more than the original and $32.49 more than the color-screen model, yet states fewer washing programs — six — than the color-screen model's eight. You are not paying more for more programs. You're paying for the 14.6-inch body, specifically, and whether that's worth $32 to $47 depends entirely on whether your counter actually needs it — a question we get to in the next section.

Listing one

The original listing's glass door, and what it doesn't say

The cheapest of the three at $284.99, the original Hermitlux countertop model is sold under a Glass Door variant — meaning the listing itself categorizes "Glass Door" as the defining attribute of this particular version, the way another seller might categorize a variant by color. A viewing window on a countertop dishwasher is a real, specific convenience: you can see whether the cycle is running, whether it has reached the rinse stage, and whether anything inside has shifted and blocked a spray arm, all without opening the door and losing the heat and pressure already built up inside the tub.

What the listing does not state is a washing-program count or a place-setting number. That is worth calling out plainly rather than papering over: the color-screen listing states eight programs and five place settings in its own title, and the Slim listing states six programs. The original listing states neither. We are not going to guess at a number the manufacturer didn't publish on this ASIN — if program count or place-setting capacity matters to your decision, that is a question for the listing's own spec section or Hermitlux's customer service, not something this page should invent to make the comparison tidier.

It's also worth noting what's missing by omission: the title doesn't mention a color screen or an LED light, the two features the newer two listings each call out. That doesn't mean the original lacks a basic control panel or interior light — it means the listing doesn't claim either upgrade as a selling point, a reasonable signal it predates them as features Hermitlux chose to market on the newer two SKUs.

Where this listing wins is straightforward: it's the cheapest entry into the lineup, and the glass door is the one feature it states clearly and specifically. If what you want is to glance at a running cycle rather than wait blind for a beep, this is the listing built around exactly that, at the lowest price of the three.

Listing two

The Slim model's 14.6-inch body: who actually needs it

The Slim model is sold under a Narrow-Space Design variant, built around a body roughly 14.6 inches wide — noticeably narrower than the countertop dishwashers most listings in this category describe, which commonly run 16 to 18 inches across. On a counter where every inch is already claimed by a coffee maker, a knife block and a dish rack, that difference is the entire reason to consider this SKU over the other two. A countertop appliance that doesn't fit the counter isn't a compromise; it's a machine you can't use. It isn't the only thing the listing states. Six washing programs, a glass door — matching the original listing's defining feature, this time as one bullet among several rather than the headline — an LED light, and the same 5-liter built-in water tank as both other models. "Easy Operation" appears in the title too, language common enough across this category that it's worth treating as marketing framing rather than a measurable spec.

Here is the honest tension in this listing: it is the most expensive of the three at $332.48, and it states fewer washing programs than the mid-priced color-screen model's eight. If your counter has room to spare, there's no spec-sheet reason to pay this premium over the color-screen model — you'd be giving up two stated programs for a body you didn't need to be narrow. The calculation only favors the Slim model when the extra inches genuinely don't fit anywhere else: a galley kitchen, a narrow counter run, a cart sized for something smaller. Measure your available width first — this is the one listing where the headline spec is physical clearance, not a feature count.

Listing three

The color-screen model's efficiency claims, read against the spec sheet

The color-screen listing states the most of the three, specifically: five place settings, eight washing programs, a color screen display, the same 5-liter built-in water tank, and "No Hookup Needed" — language that reads as a tank-only design, meant to run entirely from the built-in reservoir rather than a faucet connection. It's marketed for apartments and RVs, the two settings where a sink hookup is either unavailable or not worth the hassle of installing. At $299.99 it sits between the other two on price, and on stated program count it's the strongest listing of the three — eight programs beats the Slim model's six, and the original listing states no comparable number at all.

Call this listing's "efficiency claim" what it actually is: a program count and a feature list, not an EPA-style certification or a published energy-use figure. Nothing in the title or the specs given here states an ENERGY STAR rating, a kWh-per-cycle number, or a water-use-per-cycle figure, and we won't credit the listing with efficiency credentials it doesn't publish. Eight washing programs typically means more ways to match a cycle to a load, from a quick rinse to a heavy-soil run — but that is not the same claim as "uses less water" or "uses less electricity," and a shopper comparing this against a genuinely ENERGY STAR-certified machine from another brand should read the fine print rather than assume parity.

What the listing does not state is a glass door. Unlike its two siblings, nothing here claims a viewing window — check the current listing's photos directly rather than assuming. What you're actually buying, based on what's stated, is the most granular program selection of the three plus a color display to navigate it, aimed at no-hookup living situations. For more on how "no hookup needed" claims hold up in practice, see our guide to faucet adapters and hose kits for a portable dishwasher — useful even for a tank-only model, since most households eventually want a faucet option as backup.

The marketing claim

"Dry Tank System" versus a standard tank: does the claim hold up

A phrase that shows up on some Hermitlux listings elsewhere in this category is "Dry Tank System" — marketing language for a design that's supposed to drain or air out residual water from the built-in tank between cycles, rather than leaving a few ounces sitting in a sealed reservoir until the next wash. It's worth addressing directly here, because none of the three product titles given above uses that exact phrase. Rather than assume it applies to these specific ASINs, the honest approach is to say plainly: check the current listing's own bullet points for the words "Dry Tank" before assuming a specific one of these three machines has it, because a title that doesn't mention it on the page you're buying from is the only reliable signal.

What's worth examining is the problem the claim is aimed at — a real, general fact about any 5-liter built-in tank, Hermitlux or otherwise. A sealed reservoir that holds standing water between uses is exactly the kind of enclosed, damp space where odor develops, the same reason a drain hose on any countertop dishwasher needs occasional cleaning (a related case of a named feature worth checking against the specific listing is covered in our piece on which portable dishwasher models actually have leak detection). A tank that genuinely drains itself after each cycle solves a real nuisance; one that merely claims to, with no stated mechanism, is worth treating skeptically.

Our position: if a specific listing states a Dry Tank System or equivalent language, that's a genuine point in its favor, because residual water is a documented nuisance in small sealed reservoirs. If the listing you're buying doesn't state it — as none of the three titles above do — don't pay a premium for a feature you'd only be assuming exists. Compare the live listing's own bullets against the price, the same way you'd compare the glass door, the program count or the body width.

The decision

Which one matches your kitchen

Start with the question the glass-door listing answers, since it's the easiest to rule in or out: do you want to see the cycle without opening the door? If yes, and your counter has normal clearance, the original listing at $284.99 gives you that at the lowest price of the three — accept that you won't have a published program count to compare against the others.

Next, measure your counter. If a standard countertop dishwasher — 16 to 18 inches wide on most listings in this category — genuinely doesn't fit your available run, the Slim model's roughly 14.6-inch body is the only one built to solve that problem, worth the $32-to-$47 premium if the alternative physically can't sit where you need it. If your counter isn't actually that tight, skip this one: you'd be paying for width you don't need and losing two washing programs in the trade.

If neither the window nor the width decides it, the color-screen model is the strongest spec sheet for the money: five place settings and eight washing programs, aimed at tank-only use in an apartment or RV, at $299.99 — less than the Slim model despite more stated programs. Just don't expect a glass door; this listing doesn't claim one.

And whichever you pick, don't take a "Dry Tank System" or similar drainage claim on faith unless the specific listing you're ordering states it. Check the live bullet points, not the brand's reputation from a different SKU.

FAQ

Common questions about the Hermitlux lineup

Do any of the three Hermitlux listings connect to a kitchen faucet?

The color-screen listing specifically markets itself as needing "no hookup," which reads as tank-only operation from its built-in 5-liter reservoir. The original and Slim listings also center on the same 5-liter built-in tank in their titles, with no faucet-connection feature stated in any of the three. Treat all three as tank-fed machines unless the specific listing you're viewing states a faucet or inlet-hose option.

What is a "Dry Tank System," and do these three listings have one?

It's marketing language, used on some Hermitlux listings elsewhere in the category, for a tank designed to drain or dry out residual water between cycles rather than leaving standing water in a sealed reservoir. None of the three product titles given here uses that exact phrase, so don't assume a specific ASIN has it — check that listing's own bullet points before crediting it with the feature.

Is the Slim model's narrower body a real trade-off, or just marketing?

It's real on both sides. The roughly 14.6-inch body is narrower than the 16-to-18-inch width common on countertop dishwashers in this category, which matters if your counter genuinely can't fit a standard-width machine. The trade-off is also real: the Slim listing states six washing programs against the color-screen model's eight, and costs $32.49 more, so the narrower body is the only reason to choose it over the cheaper, more fully specified color-screen listing.

Which Hermitlux listing is the cheapest way into the lineup?

The original Glass Door model at $284.99 is the lowest-priced of the three. It's also the listing that states the least in terms of program count or place settings, so the lower price buys the viewing window specifically, not a documented spec advantage over the other two.
