Same dishwasher, two different prices.

Search Amazon for the SPT SD-9254SSB and you can land on two listings that read like the same product page copied twice — same name, same size, same stainless tub — priced $40 apart. Neither is a typo. Here's what's actually going on, how to check it yourself in under a minute, and which listing to buy.

This isn't a rare glitch. If you shop appliances on Amazon for long enough you'll run into a listing that looks like a mirror image of one you saw an hour ago — same title down to the punctuation, same bullet points, same product photos, different ASIN, different price. With the SPT SD-9254SSB, a compact 18-inch built-in dishwasher meant to slide under a counter, it's easy to prove: one listing (ASIN B07RC27S9G) is priced at $461.99, and another listing for a dishwasher with the exact same name (ASIN B09V9Z8HCX) is priced at $498.64 — a $40.45 gap, roughly 8.8 percent, for what the titles claim is the identical machine.

We're not going to tell you that's always harmless, and we're not going to tell you it's always a scam. It's a structural feature of how Amazon's catalog works, especially for appliance brands that sell through more than one storefront or fulfillment path. What matters to you as a buyer is being able to tell, in under a minute, whether two listings really are the same product before you pay the higher one by mistake — or dismiss the cheaper one as "too good to be true" when it's exactly the same machine.

The mechanism

How a listing like this happens on Amazon

A single retail listing on Amazon is tied to one ASIN, and in theory a manufacturer's catalog maps one physical product to one ASIN. In practice, appliance categories are some of the worst offenders for breaking that rule, for a handful of well-documented reasons that have nothing to do with the specific brand being shady:

  • Multiple sellers, multiple listings. A brand like SPT sells through its own storefront and through third-party resellers and distributors. If a reseller creates a new product page instead of attaching their offer to the manufacturer's existing one — sometimes because Amazon's "same product" matching didn't catch it, sometimes because the reseller wants their own reviews and Buy Box — the result is two live ASINs for one physical item.
  • Old listings that never got merged. When a model is re-photographed, re-boxed, or re-submitted with slightly different bullet copy, Amazon's catalog system sometimes treats it as a new product rather than recognizing it as the same one. The old ASIN doesn't disappear; it just sits there, sometimes with stale pricing, sometimes competing directly with the new one.
  • Fulfillment and warehouse differences. Two ASINs for the same item can be priced differently because one ships from a different fulfillment center, carries different warehousing or freight cost, or is sold by a different party paying a different wholesale price — all of which is invisible on the product page itself.
  • Bundle and packaging variants that don't announce themselves. Occasionally a higher-priced ASIN includes something the cheaper one doesn't — an extra fitting, a different power cord region, faster shipping eligibility — without the title reflecting it. This is the one case where the higher price can be justified, and it's exactly why checking rather than assuming matters.

None of this requires bad faith. It's simply what happens when a manufacturer's catalog, several resellers' inventory feeds, and Amazon's own product-matching logic all have to agree on what counts as "the same item," and they don't always agree in time — we ran into the same reseller-driven split while mapping Danby and EdgeStar's listings for a separate comparison.

Verification

Reading the spec sheet line by line

The fastest way to confirm two listings are the same machine isn't to trust the title — titles get copied wholesale whether or not the rest of the listing matches — it's to check the fields underneath it against each other, in order:

  • Full title string. Copy both titles into a text field and compare character by character. If they match exactly, including the model code, that's the strongest single signal of a genuine duplicate rather than a variant.
  • Brand and model code. Both of these listings show brand "SPT" and model code "SD-9254SSB" with no suffix change. A variant would typically change the trailing letters (more on that below).
  • Stated size or dimension. Listing A states an 18″ size field explicitly; Listing B's size field isn't populated in our data at all, which is common for listings scraped at different times — it doesn't mean the dishwasher is a different size, since the title itself states "18″ Wide" on both. When a field is genuinely missing, treat it as unconfirmed rather than assuming either way.
  • Feature bullets, for overlap not identity. Listing A's bullets mention the installation type and the wash-program list; Listing B's bullets mention the time-delay feature and the error-alarm display. These aren't contradictions — a single real product typically ships with five or six manufacturer bullet points, and different listings (or different scrapes of the same listing) often surface a different three of them. What would be a red flag is a bullet that actively contradicts the other listing — a different place-setting count, a different tub material, a different voltage. Here, the one bullet both listings share word for word, "HEATING function during drying cycle," is the tell: it's the same manufacturer copy block.
  • Product photos. Both listings draw from the same small pool of manufacturer images of this chassis — not pixel-identical crops, but the same physical unit photographed for the same product family, which is consistent with one appliance platform licensed to two listings rather than two different dishwashers that happen to share a name.

If every one of those checks lines up and only the price (and maybe the seller) differs, you're looking at a genuine catalog duplicate, and the sane move is to buy the cheaper one. If even one of them contradicts — a different place-setting count, a tub material that doesn't match, a width that's off by more than rounding — stop and treat them as two different products, whatever the title says. The trap

SD-9254 vs SD-9263: the pattern that trips people up

Here's where it gets genuinely confusing, because SPT's own model-numbering pattern looks almost designed to be misread at a glance. The two ASINs above are both "SD-9254SSB" — same four-digit chassis code, same trim letters. But search a little further and you'll also find the SD-9263SSA, a dishwasher from the same brand, in the same finish, at a similar price point, with a model number one digit removed from the first pair. It is a different machine, not a third listing of the same one.

The title tells you so if you read past the brand name: the SD-9254SSB is described as "18″ Wide Built-In Stainless Steel Dishwasher," built to install top-mount under a counter with a separate panel. The SD-9263SSA is described as "18″ Wide Portable Stainless Steel Dishwasher" — a freestanding chassis in the same family that SPT also sells on castors in other trims, the same portable-class platform covered in our countertop-vs-18-inch comparison. Both carry 8 place settings, 6 wash programs and ENERGY STAR certification, which is exactly why the two chassis get confused — the spec summary reads almost the same even though the installation method doesn't.

The practical rule: the four-digit chassis code (9254 versus 9263) tells you which physical dishwasher you're looking at — built-in versus portable — and that's not negotiable between listings. The trailing letters (SSA, SSB, SSC and so on) typically mark finish or minor trim variants within the same chassis, the way SPT's portable line also appears as SD-9263W in white and SD-9263SSC in stainless in other listings we've tracked. Two listings sharing the full code including the trailing letters, like the pair at the top of this page, are candidates for a true duplicate. Two listings differing in the chassis digits are not — they're different appliances that happen to share a brand, a finish and a marketing paragraph.

Before you click buy

A checklist for spotting a duplicate listing

01

Match the full title

Copy both titles and compare them character by character, including the model code. An exact match is the strongest signal of a true duplicate.

02

Check the chassis digits

On SPT's catalog and similar appliance brands, the core numeric code (9254, 9263, and so on) identifies the physical platform. A different code is a different machine, full stop.

03

Scan for a contradicting bullet

A different place-setting count, tub material, or dimension in the feature list overrides a matching title. Treat it as a different product.

04

Compare photos, not thumbnails

Open the full image set on both listings. The same manufacturer photo pool, reused across listings, supports a duplicate; entirely different staging photos are a flag to slow down.

If all four checks line up, you're very likely looking at the same physical dishwasher sold under two ASINs, and price is the only variable left to decide on.

FAQ

Common questions

Are B07RC27S9G and B09V9Z8HCX actually the same dishwasher?

Based on the published data for both listings, yes: identical title text, identical model code (SD-9254SSB), identical stated capacity and wash-program count, and feature bullets that overlap rather than contradict. The only way to be completely certain before you buy is to open both Amazon listings yourself and run the same checks, since a title match alone doesn't guarantee every detail of a live listing.

Why does Amazon let the same product have two ASINs and two prices?

Because a single ASIN is supposed to map to one unique product, but in practice gets created independently by different sellers, different fulfillment setups, or catalog entries that were never merged after a re-listing. Appliance categories see this often because manufacturers sell through their own storefront and through multiple resellers and distributors at once.

Is the SPT SD-9263SSA a cheaper alternative to the SD-9254SSB?

No — it's a different installation class, not a cheaper version of the same machine. The SD-9254SSB is a built-in, top-mount dishwasher designed to slide under a counter; the SD-9263SSA is a portable, freestanding dishwasher from the same brand's wheeled product line. They happen to share a similar place-setting count and finish, which is exactly why the model numbers get confused.

Which price should I pay for the SD-9254SSB?

Whichever listing is cheaper, once you've confirmed the title, model code, feature bullets and photos all match — which they do for the two ASINs compared on this page. Absent a stated difference in what ships in the box, paying more for an identical listing buys you nothing.
