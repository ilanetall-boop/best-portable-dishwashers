One badge, two BLACK+DECKER dishwashers. Same brand, different kitchen.

The badge

One Energy Star certification, two completely different machines

Energy Star certification tells you a machine met a federal efficiency threshold for water and electricity use per cycle — it says nothing about size, price or where the appliance belongs in your home. All three BLACK+DECKER models carry it, and that's precisely why the badge is the wrong place to start shopping. A buyer who filters by "Energy Star certified, BLACK+DECKER" gets a $299 counter appliance and a $610 floor appliance in the same results, with no signal from the certification itself about which one will fit.

What actually separates them is class, not efficiency. The BCD6W and BCD6SD are both sealed countertop tubs that sit on your worktop and connect to a kitchen faucet; both claim 6-place-setting capacity. The BPD8B is a freestanding unit on castors that stands on the floor, rolls to the sink, and claims 8 place settings — closer to what a built-in dishwasher holds. Same brand, same badge, two different product lines entirely.

That split matters because it decides the comparison you're actually making. BCD6W against BCD6SD is a same-price, same-capacity choice about one interior material. BPD8B against either countertop model is a different question: is the extra capacity and the floor-standing form worth $310 and a change in how the appliance occupies your kitchen. Treat them as two separate decisions, not one three-way shootout, and the badge stops being confusing.

The countertop twins

BCD6W vs BCD6SD: what the stainless interior actually adds

At $299.23 and $299.99 — a 76-cent gap — the BCD6W and BCD6SD are priced as if BLACK+DECKER wants you to pick on specification, not on cost. Both are white countertop machines, both claim 6-place-setting capacity. The differences that exist are specific and worth naming individually rather than assuming "stainless must mean more."

  • Interior material. The BCD6SD's listing title leads with "Stainless Steel Interior" — the one feature it's built around. The BCD6W's title doesn't make that claim, so we don't assert a stainless tub for it; treat its interior as the manufacturer's standard finish unless the current listing says otherwise.
  • Wash programs. The BCD6W's title states 7 Wash Programs. The BCD6SD's title states 6. That's a real difference in the BCD6W's favor on paper — one more program on the dial, not a trivial rounding difference.
  • Delay start. The BCD6W's title specifically calls out a 24-Hour Delayed Start. The BCD6SD's title does not mention a delay-start feature at all. If a delayed start — load it at breakfast, run it overnight — is something you'll actually use, that's a point in the BCD6W's favor that the price tag alone won't tell you.

So the honest framing is this: a stainless interior is generally understood to resist staining and odor retention better than a painted or plastic tub over years of use, which is a real durability argument for the BCD6SD. But it isn't a free upgrade — on the specs each listing actually states, the BCD6SD gives up one wash program and the delay-start timer to get it, at essentially the same price. Whether that trade is worth it depends on whether you care more about tub longevity or about the scheduling flexibility and extra cycle option.

This is exactly the kind of same-brand, near-identical-price confusion that trips up buyers across the whole category, not just BLACK+DECKER — our guide to why the same portable dishwasher shows up at two different prices on Amazon walks through how to read listing titles for this kind of SKU-level difference before you add to cart.

The 18-inch jump

The BPD8B: 8 place settings and what the extra $310 buys

The BPD8B is a different product line, not a bigger version of the countertop machines. It's a genuine 18-inch freestanding dishwasher on castors, finished in black, carrying the same Energy Star certification as the two countertop twins — and priced at $609.99, roughly $310 more than either one.

What that premium buys, going by its published specification: two more place settings (8 against 6), which in practice is the difference between a full day's dishes for two or three people and a genuine family load with room for a pan. It also buys a change in where the machine lives — the BPD8B stands on the floor on castors rather than occupying 16-plus inches of your worktop permanently, and it rolls to the sink and back rather than sitting fixed beside it. For a kitchen with little spare counter but a bit of floor to spare, that's a meaningful trade even before capacity enters the conversation.

What it doesn't buy is a water tank: like the countertop twins, the BPD8B connects to a faucet rather than filling from a jug, so a kitchen tap with a removable aerator is still a requirement regardless of which of the three models you choose. And at nearly double the combined price of the two countertop machines, the BPD8B only earns its premium if you actually need those extra two place settings and the floor-standing form factor — a single person or couple rarely fills a 6-place countertop machine in the first place, let alone an 8-place one.

The jump from a 6-place countertop machine to an 8-place freestanding one is a smaller version of a bigger pattern in this category — our comparison of 18-inch portables against full-size 24-inch built-ins covers what happens if 8 place settings still isn't enough and a built-in becomes the realistic next step.

Programs & timers

Programs and delay-start timers, compared across both classes

Lined up by what each listing actually states, the program counts tell a small but real story:

  • BCD6W — 7 Wash Programs, 24-Hour Delayed Start. The most programs of the three, and the only one of the three whose listing states a delay-start feature at all.
  • BCD6SD — 6 Wash Programs. One fewer program than the BCD6W, and no delay-start feature stated in its title.
  • BPD8B — the listing we're working from states Energy Star certification and 8 place settings but does not enumerate individual wash-program names or a delay-start figure. We don't invent one: if cycle selection or scheduling flexibility matters to you on the 18-inch model, check the current Amazon listing or the product manual for the exact program list before buying.

The pattern worth noticing is that more capacity didn't buy more visible programming here — the cheapest of the three, the BCD6W, is the one whose own title makes the strongest claim on both program count and scheduling. That's a useful reminder for this whole category: price tracks capacity and build, not necessarily feature count on the control panel. If a delayed start is a feature you'll actually use — loading the machine before work and timing it to finish in the evening, say — the BCD6W is the one model here whose listing commits to it in writing.

A delay-start timer only matters if the machine is also connected and ready to run unattended, which for any faucet-fed unit means a steady water supply at the tap. If your home runs on well water or weak municipal pressure, that's worth checking before you set a schedule and walk away — see our guide to portable dishwashers for low water pressure or well water for what that constraint actually does to a wash cycle.

Which BLACK+DECKER model actually fits a small-kitchen budget

FAQ

Common questions about the BCD6W, BCD6SD and BPD8B

What's the real difference between the BLACK+DECKER BCD6W and BCD6SD?

Both are white countertop dishwashers claiming 6 place settings at essentially the same price. The BCD6SD's listing is built around a stainless steel interior; the BCD6W's listing states 7 wash programs and a 24-hour delayed start, while the BCD6SD's listing states 6 programs and does not mention a delay-start feature. Neither listing claims the other's standout spec, so the choice comes down to whether you value the stainless interior or the extra program and the timer more.

Is the BLACK+DECKER BPD8B worth $310 more than the countertop models?

Only if you need the extra capacity or the floor-standing form. The BPD8B claims 8 place settings against 6 for either countertop twin, and it stands on castors rather than occupying worktop space — real advantages for a household of three or more with little spare counter. For one or two people, a 6-place countertop machine at roughly half the price typically covers the same daily dish load.

Does the BLACK+DECKER BPD8B have a delay-start timer?

The listing we worked from states 8 place settings and Energy Star certification but does not enumerate individual wash programs or a delay-start figure the way the BCD6W's listing does. We don't assert a feature that isn't stated — check the current Amazon listing or product manual for the exact program and timer details before buying if that matters to you.

Do all three BLACK+DECKER models connect to a kitchen faucet?

Yes. None of the three — BCD6W, BCD6SD or BPD8B — is described as having a built-in water tank in the specifications we worked from; all three are faucet-connected. A kitchen tap with a removable aerator and standard threads is a requirement across the whole lineup, regardless of which model or price point you choose.
