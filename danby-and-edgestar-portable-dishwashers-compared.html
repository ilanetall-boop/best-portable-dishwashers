Danby and EdgeStar, compared honestly.

COMFEE' and EUHOMY own most roundups in this category, including ours. Danby and EdgeStar barely show up, and not because their machines are worse — a wheeled 18-inch Danby, a wide EdgeStar countertop and an EdgeStar built-in sit in three different corners of the market that a "best overall" list isn't built to cover.

Search "best portable dishwasher" and the same six or seven brand names cycle through every list: COMFEE', EUHOMY, BLACK+DECKER, GE Profile, NOVETE. Danby and EdgeStar are real appliance brands with real distribution — Danby is a long-running Canadian manufacturer of compact and specialty appliances, EdgeStar a US brand built around small-footprint kitchen and bar equipment — and each sells a portable dishwasher that would fit cleanly into most of those lists. They just don't, for reasons that have more to do with how roundups get built than with the machines themselves.

This page covers three specific products: the Danby DDW1805EWP, an 18-inch freestanding dishwasher on wheels; the EdgeStar DWP63, a 21-5/8-inch countertop unit; and the EdgeStar BIDW1802SS, an 18-inch dishwasher built to install permanently rather than roll around. They aren't three versions of one machine — they're three different answers to "where does this go in my kitchen," from a pair of brands most comparison pages skip past.

The gap

Why these two brands keep getting left off the list

It isn't an accident, and it isn't a quality judgment either. Three mechanical reasons explain most of it.

First, volume. COMFEE' and EUHOMY sell countertop dishwashers at a scale that makes them the default comparison set in the affiliate-network product feeds most sites build from. Danby and EdgeStar list fewer total SKUs in this specific category, so they surface less often in a scrape of best-sellers.

Second, category confusion. A "best portable dishwasher" list is almost always implicitly a best-countertop-dishwasher list, because that's where the volume is. The Danby DDW1805EWP is a freestanding 18-inch unit on wheels — a different machine class entirely, closer to our own countertop-vs-18-inch comparison than to a six-place countertop tub. Roundups built around one format quietly exclude the other.

Third, EdgeStar sells across two of those formats under one brand name — a countertop unit and a built-in — which doesn't fit neatly into a "countertop" list or a "freestanding" list. A product that straddles categories tends to get cut from both.

None of that means skip them. It means the usual roundup logic doesn't apply, and the right question is narrower: does this specific machine solve your specific installation problem.

Danby

The Danby DDW1805EWP: quick-connect at 18 inches wide

The DDW1805EWP is an 18-inch wide, freestanding portable dishwasher finished in white, built to the same basic format as the BLACK+DECKER and SPT machines covered in our countertop-vs-18-inch comparison: full height, on castors, with a finished top surface and a quick-connect hookup onto a standard kitchen faucet, no plumbing modification required. Roll it to the sink, connect the coupler, run the cycle, roll it back against the wall when it's done.

The spec sheet available to us lists 8 place settings — in the same range as the 18-inch class generally, and close to what a standard built-in dishwasher holds. A place setting is a defined unit (one dinner plate, one side plate or bowl, one glass, one cup and saucer, one person's cutlery, roughly ten items), so eight settings means this machine clears a real family's dinner dishes in one cycle rather than forcing a second run.

What we don't have for this unit is a confirmed current price. Unlike the two EdgeStar models below, where our database has a specific figure, the Danby's listing price should be checked live on Amazon before you factor it into a decision — the rest of the 18-inch class, per our comparison piece, runs from roughly $590 to $690.

The quick-connect hookup is worth dwelling on, because it's the single biggest practical difference between this class of machine and a countertop tank model. A quick-connect coupler attaches to a faucet with a removable aerator and standard threads — most straight kitchen taps qualify, but pull-down and pull-out sprayer faucets generally don't, because the spray head unscrews from a hose rather than sitting on a fixed spout. If your faucet is a sprayer type, the DDW1805EWP is off the table regardless of price, and a tank-fed countertop unit becomes the only version of this appliance that works in your kitchen.

EdgeStar, countertop

The EdgeStar DWP63: 21-5/8 inches wide, and a child lock

The EdgeStar DWP63 is a countertop dishwasher — a sealed tub format, not the freestanding-on-wheels class the Danby belongs to — finished in black, with a 21-5/8-inch width that puts it at the large end of the countertop category. For comparison, the widest countertop machine in our main guide, the COMFEE' six-place model, is 21.7 inches wide; the DWP63 sits almost exactly alongside it. That width buys interior capacity, at the cost of eating closer to two feet of counter real estate than the 17-inch footprint of a compact tank model.

Capacity is rated at 6 place settings, the same figure as the COMFEE' flagship and a meaningful step up from the 4-setting machines that dominate the compact end of the countertop class. Six settings is roughly the point where a countertop dishwasher stops being a gadget for one or two people and starts clearing a full day's dishes for a small household in a single cycle.

The feature that sets the DWP63 apart from most countertop machines we've covered elsewhere on this site is a child lock, which prevents the control panel or door from being operated without first disabling it — not something COMFEE', EUHOMY or NOVETE's countertop listings call out in our database. If there are young children in the kitchen and the dishwasher sits at counter height, well within reach, that's a genuinely different consideration than it is for a floor-level built-in.

Our database lists the DWP63 at $349.00, above the $270–$330 range most compact countertop machines occupy. The extra cost tracks with the extra width and capacity rather than reading as a brand premium — six settings at $349 works out to a comparable per-setting price to the COMFEE' six-place unit at roughly $279.

No Size/variant line is published for the DWP63 beyond its width and capacity figures above; we aren't asserting a specific plate diameter. Check the current Amazon listing for plate-size specifics before buying if your household runs larger dinnerware.

EdgeStar, built-in

The EdgeStar BIDW1802SS: when the built-in model makes more sense than the countertop one

The BIDW1802SS is the odd one out in EdgeStar's own lineup, and the reason this comparison exists in the first place: an 18-inch wide built-in dishwasher, finished in stainless steel, designed to install permanently into a cabinet opening rather than sit freestanding or roll on wheels. It's rated for 8 place settings — the same as the Danby, double the DWP63's countertop capacity — because a built-in installation gives up portability for a full-height tub that isn't constrained by a countertop machine's shallow footprint.

The decision between EdgeStar's own countertop DWP63 and its built-in BIDW1802SS is a decision about your kitchen, not the brand. The built-in model needs a dedicated cabinet opening with supply and drain lines run to it — an existing cutout, or a willingness to modify cabinetry. If you're renting and can't modify the kitchen, the BIDW1802SS is very likely the wrong choice regardless of specs; that's the territory covered by this site's broader portable dishwasher buyer's guide.

But if you own the space, or you're replacing a dishwasher with a cutout and lines already in place, the built-in format solves problems the other two both have: no counter space lost, and no faucet adapter depending on a removable aerator — the exact failure point flagged for the Danby above.

Our database lists the BIDW1802SS at $539.00 — roughly $190 above the DWP63, below the $590–$690 range the freestanding 18-inch class occupies. That price doesn't include installation: a built-in dishwasher needing new supply and drain lines run to a cabinet opening carries labor cost on top of the unit price, so treat $539 as the floor, not the ceiling.

No Size/variant specification beyond the 18-inch width and 8-setting capacity is published in our source data, so we aren't asserting plate diameter. Confirm cabinet-opening dimensions against the current listing before committing — a built-in dishwasher is far more expensive to return than a countertop or wheeled unit.

After the sale

Parts and support: what happens when something breaks

This is the honest trade-off of buying outside the handful of brands that dominate every roundup. COMFEE' and EUHOMY sell at high enough volume that third-party parts listings and troubleshooting threads build up around specific models quickly. A smaller-volume brand selling fewer units in this exact category doesn't generate that same secondary ecosystem as fast.

That doesn't mean Danby or EdgeStar lack support — both are established manufacturers with their own warranty and parts channels, different from being an unknown seller. The practical difference shows up less in whether you can get a part, and more in how: expect to go through the manufacturer's warranty service rather than a generic replacement on a general appliance-parts marketplace, the way you often can for a BLACK+DECKER or GE unit.

This matters most for the EdgeStar BIDW1802SS. A wheeled unit like the Danby can be unplugged, boxed and shipped to a service center under warranty. A built-in dishwasher already plumbed into a cabinet opening is harder to ship anywhere — a service call is the realistic path, so check EdgeStar's warranty terms and local service availability before buying that specific model.

None of this is a reason to avoid either brand. It's a reason to read the warranty terms before buying, and to keep original packaging until you're confident the unit works through at least one full cycle — the subject of our guide to running a new portable dishwasher empty before first use.

The decision

Who should actually buy Danby or EdgeStar over the bigger names

Or a specific feature decides it, like the DWP63's child lock. Or you're replacing a built-in that already has plumbing in place and want a straightforward 8-setting unit rather than a smart or app-connected one.

Stick with COMFEE', EUHOMY or BLACK+DECKER if

Any one of these is true

Or price is the only variable that matters and you want the widest possible field to compare against.

The honest summary: Danby and EdgeStar aren't missing from most roundups because they're worse machines. They're missing because a "best overall" list has to pick one format and a handful of best-selling brands within it, and these three products span three different formats under two brands that don't dominate any single one by volume. If your kitchen's constraints point at a wheeled 18-inch unit, a wide countertop machine with a child lock, or a permanent built-in replacement, these three are worth comparing directly against the category leaders on spec and price.

FAQ

Common questions about Danby and EdgeStar dishwashers

Why don't Danby and EdgeStar dishwashers show up in most "best portable dishwasher" lists?

Mostly volume and category mismatch, not quality. COMFEE' and EUHOMY sell countertop dishwashers at a scale that dominates the product feeds most roundups are built from, and a "portable dishwasher" list is usually implicitly a countertop-dishwasher list. Danby's main entry here is a freestanding 18-inch wheeled unit, a different format entirely, and EdgeStar sells across two formats under one brand name, which tends to get it excluded from lists built around a single format.

What's the difference between the EdgeStar DWP63 and the EdgeStar BIDW1802SS?

The DWP63 is a 21-5/8-inch countertop dishwasher rated for 6 place settings that sits on a counter and connects to a faucet with no installation beyond that. The BIDW1802SS is an 18-inch built-in dishwasher rated for 8 place settings that installs permanently into a cabinet opening with dedicated water supply and drain lines, like a standard full-size dishwasher. They're sold by the same brand but solve different installation problems.

Does the Danby DDW1805EWP need a special faucet?

It connects with a quick-connect coupler that attaches to a faucet with a removable aerator and standard threads, the same requirement as other 18-inch wheeled dishwashers in this category. Pull-down and pull-out sprayer faucets generally cannot accept that type of adapter because the spray head unscrews from a hose rather than sitting on a fixed spout — if that describes your kitchen's faucet, a wheeled 18-inch machine isn't a workable option regardless of brand.

Is it harder to get parts for a Danby or EdgeStar dishwasher than for COMFEE' or EUHOMY?

Likely yes, in the sense that lower sales volume in this specific category means less of a secondary parts and troubleshooting ecosystem has built up around specific models. Both are established appliance manufacturers with their own warranty and parts channels, so support exists — it's just more likely to run through the manufacturer directly rather than a generic third-party parts marketplace. For the built-in EdgeStar model specifically, checking in-home service availability before buying is worth the extra step.
