EUHOMY's portable dishwashers. Seven listings, one chassis.

Search "EUHOMY portable dishwasher" on Amazon and you'll get seven listings back, most of them quoting the same 40 dB rating and the same 5-liter tank language in a different order. EUHOMY is the clearest case in this category of what shopping for a portable dishwasher actually involves. The brand runs at least seven active countertop dishwasher listings on Amazon at once, and from the product photos alone you'd be forgiven for assuming they're the same machine with different stock numbers. Most quote a 40 dB noise rating, mention a 5-liter tank and "no hookup needed," and describe dual spray arms and a program count between six and eight. The copy reads like it was written once and reused with minor edits — because, in every case we could verify, it was.

We limited this comparison to the four EUHOMY listings in our product database with verified ASINs, prices and specification text — we won't invent a spec for the other SKUs circulating on Amazon just to round the number out to seven. What follows is what those four titles actually say, where they genuinely overlap, where one quietly drops a claim the others make, and the single spec that's only true of one of the four.

Why this happens

Why one brand can have seven listings that read almost the same

This isn't unique to EUHOMY, and it isn't necessarily a scam — it's how a category this young gets manufactured. A single factory chassis gets certified once, then a brand lists it under several SKUs to hold more search real estate, test price points, or split inventory across fulfillment batches with slightly different accessory bundles. We've documented the same pattern with Silonn's two countertop dishwashers and Hamilton Beach's three-model lineup. EUHOMY simply runs more SKUs through it than either.

The practical effect on a shopper is that search results stop sorting by "which machine is better" and start sorting by "which listing has the best photos or the lowest price today." Scanning four nearly identical title strings for the words that actually differ is tedious, which is why most buyers don't do it and end up picking whichever one Amazon's algorithm put first. If a no-name listing is more your concern than a crowded brand lineup, our look at generic-brand portable dishwashers covers the opposite problem: too little verifiable information rather than too much repetition.

To be fair to EUHOMY, the underlying hardware isn't fake or defective — it's a real, fairly mature compact-countertop platform. The issue for a buyer isn't quality, it's redundancy: paying $280 for a listing that offers nothing a $249 listing from the same brand doesn't, because nobody read all four title strings side by side before clicking "buy now."

The overlap

Tank size, program count and 40 dB: the specs repeated across nearly every version

Three claims show up, in some combination, on three of the four listings we verified: a 40 dB noise rating, a 5-liter built-in tank, and "no hookup needed" phrasing that tells you the machine runs from that tank rather than a faucet connection. The 6 Programs with 40 dB, No Hookup listing (ASIN B0CVX5GBY5, $249.00) states the 40 dB figure and the no-hookup claim, though not a tank size. The 8-Program, 167°F Wash listing (ASIN B0HFKMLQDN, $267.36) states all three: 5-liter tank, 40 dB, no hookup needed. The 8 Programs with 5L Tank, No Hookup listing (ASIN B0HJQCBV4H, $279.99) also states all three, plus several specs none of the others mention.

Program count is the one spec that visibly splits the four into two camps rather than one. Two listings advertise 6 programs — the $249.00 model and the EUHOMY Countertop Dishwasher, 6 Programs, Dual Spray Arms for 30+ Items (ASIN B0FL7FHGTC, $279.99) — and two advertise 8 programs. Within a brand family, program count is usually the first and most reliable signal of which tier a listing sits in, more reliable than price alone, because (as this lineup shows) a 6-program listing and an 8-program listing can carry near-identical prices.

What's notable is what the fourth listing, the $279.99 six-program model, doesn't say. Its title mentions dual spray arms and a "30+ items" capacity claim, matching language used elsewhere in the lineup, but it states no decibel figure and no tank size at all. We don't know whether that's because the spec wasn't tested, wasn't disclosed, or genuinely differs — and if the listing doesn't say it, we don't say it either. That gap is itself useful: it's the one listing in this group where the shared language breaks down instead of repeating.

What's actually different

The 29-minute quick wash: the one spec unique to a single listing

Across all four titles, exactly one spec appears on exactly one listing and nowhere else: a 29-minute quick-wash program, stated in the title of the EUHOMY Countertop Dishwasher Portable, 8 Programs with 5L Tank, No Hookup (ASIN B0HJQCBV4H, $279.99). None of the other three EUHOMY titles in this comparison mention a quick-wash cycle length at all, let alone a specific number.

A short program matters more than it sounds, since these units don't have the wash-tank volume or pump pressure of a full-size dishwasher, and a dedicated quick program exists for a half-finished sink of lightly soiled glasses rather than a full day's dishes. A machine with a named short program is solving a problem the others in this lineup don't claim to solve — worth weighing against our broader look at half-load and small-batch cycles on a portable dishwasher.

The same listing also states specs absent elsewhere in this family: child lock, a delay-start mode, and a 167°F dry temperature. Delay start lets you load the machine before bed and have it finish overnight, and child lock matters more on a countertop unit than a built-in, since the control panel sits at exactly the height a toddler can reach. None of the other three listings mention either feature — which either means those machines don't have them, or the sellers left them out of the title.

Read twice

73-hour ventilation and other claims worth reading twice

The quick-wash listing states "73h Ventilation" in its title — a specific-sounding number that on its own tells you almost nothing. It likely refers to an air-circulation feature rated to run for roughly 73 hours after a wash cycle, to keep a sealed tub from developing a musty smell between uses, a real and common problem on a small countertop dishwasher. But the listing doesn't explain the mechanism, and a number this specific attached to a feature this vague is exactly the kind of claim we'd ask you to read twice — precise-sounding marketing language, not an independently verified measurement.

The 167°F figures that show up twice in this lineup — as a wash temperature on B0HFKMLQDN, a dry temperature on B0HJQCBV4H — deserve the same scrutiny: manufacturer-stated figures for internal cycle stages, not numbers measured with a thermometer inside the tub. Treat them as directional claims, not specs you'd use to confirm sanitization. The shared 40 dB figure is more reassuring: repeated nearly verbatim across multiple SKUs, it's consistent with all of them using the same tested chassis — you're not getting four independently quiet machines, you're getting one noise profile sold four times.

None of this makes the lineup dishonest. It makes it one where the title copy does a lot of the selling, and the gap between "this machine has feature X" and "this title mentions feature X" is wider than usual. The decision

How to tell which EUHOMY listing is the better deal this month

Because EUHOMY runs four verified listings at three price points that shift independently, "which one is the best deal" has a different answer depending on the day you check. Here's the method we used, repeatable in under two minutes on Amazon.

Step one: sort by program count, not price

Note whether each listing states 6 or 8 programs — the most stable spec in the lineup, since titles get edited and prices move hourly but an 8-program listing is unlikely to quietly become a 6-program machine. Compare the two 8-program listings against each other, and the two 6-program listings against each other; comparing across tiers tells you nothing useful.

Step two: compare same-tier listings on price per stated feature

Within the 8-program pair, as of our last check B0HFKMLQDN ran $267.36 and B0HJQCBV4H ran $279.99 — a $12.63 gap for a quick-wash cycle, child lock, delay mode and the ventilation claim. If that gap holds or narrows, the quick-wash model is the better deal. If it widens past $20–25, the plainer listing becomes the more defensible buy unless you want the short cycle specifically.

Within the 6-program pair, B0CVX5GBY5 at $249.00 states a 40 dB rating and no-hookup phrasing that B0FL7FHGTC at $279.99 does not state at all, for thirty dollars less. On stated specs alone, the cheaper listing is the stronger buy unless the pricier one drops below it.

Step three: re-check the title before you click buy

Amazon listings get edited — a seller can add or drop a claim without changing the ASIN. Re-read the current title against our table above rather than assuming it's frozen in time, and buy whichever listing is best-specified for its price that day.

If what you actually want is more capacity than any countertop machine in this family offers — EUHOMY's lineup tops out at "30+ items," not a high stated place-setting count — our guide to high-capacity portable dishwashers looks past the countertop class entirely.

FAQ

Common questions about EUHOMY's lineup

Are EUHOMY's portable dishwashers all the same machine with different labels?

Not provably identical, but close enough on paper that it mostly doesn't matter. The four listings we verified share a 40 dB noise claim, 5-liter tank language and dual-spray-arm wording, with program count (6 or 8) as the clearest dividing line. One listing adds a 29-minute quick-wash cycle, child lock, delay mode and a ventilation claim the other three don't state.

What does EUHOMY's "73h Ventilation" claim actually mean?

The listing doesn't explain the mechanism, so we can't say precisely. It most likely describes an air-circulation or odor-management feature rated for roughly 73 hours after a cycle, which addresses a real issue with sealed countertop tubs — they can develop a musty smell between uses. Treat it as a manufacturer claim rather than an independently measured spec, the same way we treat the 167°F wash and dry temperatures quoted elsewhere in this lineup.

Which EUHOMY listing has the quick-wash cycle?

Only the EUHOMY Countertop Dishwasher Portable, 8 Programs with 5L Tank, No Hookup (ASIN B0HJQCBV4H) states a 29-minute quick-wash cycle in its title. None of the other three EUHOMY listings we verified mention a specific quick-wash cycle length.

Is the cheapest EUHOMY listing worth buying over the pricier ones?

If its title's two stated specs — six programs and a 40 dB rating — cover everything you need, yes: it's the lowest verified price in the lineup. If you want the 29-minute quick-wash cycle, child lock or delay mode, you'd need one of the 8-program listings, and only one of those two states the extra features. Compare program count first, then price per stated feature within the same tier, rather than ranking all four purely by price.
