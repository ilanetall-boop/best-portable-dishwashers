Comparison · One brand, three machines

Kenmore makes three very different "portable" dishwashers. Which one fits?

An $849 18-inch compact in fingerprint-resistant stainless, and two $449 24-inch hybrid-tub machines that are identical except for the color on the door. One brand badge, two completely different answers to what a kitchen actually needs — and the smaller machine is the expensive one.

By Ilane Tall · Updated November 17, 2026 · Research-based.

Search "Kenmore dishwasher" on Amazon and three listings come up that don't read like one product line. Two are 24-inch machines with a "hybrid tub," priced at $449.99 each, identical on every published spec except that one is finished in black and the other in white. The third is an 18-inch compact model, built-in, finished in fingerprint-resistant stainless steel — priced at $849.99, nearly double either 24-inch machine despite being the physically smaller appliance.

That last fact is the whole reason this page exists. In almost every other corner of this category, a bigger dishwasher costs more than a smaller one. Kenmore's own lineup runs the opposite direction: the narrower unit is the premium model, and the two wider ones are the budget pick. Below: why one badge covers an $849 compact and two $449 hybrid-tub machines, what "hybrid tub" describes, whether black-versus-white costs anything real, and which listing fits a kitchen with no existing cutout.

The badge

Why one brand name covers an $849 compact and two $449 hybrid-tub machines

Kenmore has spent decades as a full-line appliance brand sold through Sears and now relicensed across retail channels, so the name sits on products built by different manufacturing partners rather than one team iterating on a single platform. That shows here in how little the three listings share beyond the logo: different widths, different installation wording, different tub construction, and a price gap with nothing to do with physical size.

The two 24-inch listings are built around what Kenmore calls a "hybrid tub," rated at 54 dBA, with a MoreSpace adjustable rack, UltraWash and High Temp wash options, SmartWash, and 12 place settings — a genuinely large capacity for this category, priced like a mainstream big-box appliance rather than a specialty compact unit.

The 18-inch listing is a different machine on a different chassis: fingerprint-resistant stainless steel, rated at 45 dBA, built-in, with an EasyFlex 3rd rack, UltraWash Plus, TurboDry and SmartWash, and 10 place settings — two fewer than either hybrid-tub model. At $849.99 it costs roughly $400 more than either 24-inch machine despite the narrower footprint and lower rated capacity: three machines built for three jobs wearing one name tag, the same badge-covers-multiple-platforms pattern we found in Danby and EdgeStar's separate lineups.

The expensive one

The 18-inch compact: EasyFlex 3rd rack and UltraWash Plus at the $849 price point

What $849.99 buys, according to the listing itself, is a stack of features none of the 24-inch machines carry. The EasyFlex 3rd rack is a dedicated upper tier for flatware, small bowls and odd-shaped items — a rack full-size premium dishwashers have popularized and compact machines in this category rarely include. UltraWash Plus sits above the plain UltraWash system named on the 24-inch listings; the listing doesn't break down what "Plus" changes mechanically, so we won't guess beyond the name.

TurboDry is the compact's drying system, worth flagging because neither 24-inch listing names a drying feature at all — their titles stop at High Temp and SmartWash. A dedicated drying step is the difference between dry dishes and residual moisture on plastics, and that matters more on a smaller machine with less airflow for passive drying.

Then there's the finish and noise rating. Fingerprint-resistant stainless steel is a kitchen-design consideration, not a wash-performance one, but a real cost driver. At a rated 45 dBA against the hybrid tub's 54 dBA, the compact is the quieter machine by a meaningful margin — every 10 dB is roughly a doubling of perceived loudness. That explains where roughly $400 of the price gap is going: a narrower chassis, more features, a quieter motor, a premium finish — not raw capacity.

The budget pair

The 24-inch Hybrid Tub: 12 place settings, 54 dBA, and what "hybrid tub" actually means

Twelve place settings is the headline spec on both 24-inch listings — the largest capacity figure in this comparison, two more than the $849 compact. A place setting is the industry's standard unit, roughly a dinner plate, a side plate or bowl, a glass, a cup and saucer, and one person's cutlery, so 12 settings is enough for most households to run the dishwasher once a day rather than planning meals around rack space.

"Hybrid tub" is the other headline term here, and the listing itself doesn't define it. In dishwasher construction generally, "hybrid" describes a tub built from more than one material — typically pairing a more durable material, like stainless steel, where heat and water exposure is highest, with a lighter material elsewhere. That's general industry background, not a confirmed teardown of this model, since the listing names the feature but doesn't document the material split. What it does confirm: the MoreSpace rack, UltraWash and High Temp modes, and SmartWash, at a rated 54 dBA — louder on paper than the compact's 45 dBA, tracking with the larger tub volume.

The trade-off for that capacity and lower price is the absence of the compact model's extra features: no named third rack, no "Plus" wash tier, no dedicated drying step. These are large, full-capacity machines built around volume and a standard wash-and-dry cycle, not the small-footprint refinements Kenmore charges for on the narrower model.

Black or white

The picks, and which finish costs more for no functional reason

Set the two 24-inch listings side by side and there is nothing to compare except the door color. Same hybrid tub, same 54 dBA rating, same MoreSpace adjustable rack, same UltraWash, High Temp and SmartWash modes, same 12 place settings, same ENERGY STAR certification — and, as of this writing, the same $449.99 price for both the black and the white finish. Neither listing is charging a premium right now.

That matters because it isn't always true of same-model, different-finish listings — separate ASINs price independently and routinely drift apart even when nothing changes, the pattern we documented in why the same portable dishwasher shows up at two different prices. If a gap opens here later, whichever finish is priced higher isn't buying anything the spec sheet can justify.

So the honest advice for the 24-inch pair is to pick by kitchen, not by spec: match whichever color already dominates your other appliances and hardware, and treat price as the only tiebreaker if the two listings ever diverge. The 18-inch compact doesn't offer this choice — it's sold in a single fingerprint-resistant stainless finish, which is itself part of why it costs what it costs.

The inversion

Capacity versus footprint: why bigger isn't actually the premium model here

Most of this category runs on a simple rule: more place settings costs more money, since a bigger tub means more material, pump and motor. We cover that math in our general comparison of 24-inch and 18-inch capacity — where a 24-inch built-in with nearly double an 18-inch unit's place settings often costs less, not more, already running against the instinct that bigger means pricier.

Kenmore's own lineup pushes that inversion further. Here, the 24-inch Hybrid Tub holds 12 place settings against the compact's 10 — the bigger machine, by a real margin — and it's the cheaper machine by roughly $400. The explanation isn't that Kenmore prices capacity backwards; it's that capacity isn't what's being sold at the $849 price point. The compact's premium is built around fitting a quieter motor, a third rack, an upgraded wash tier and a drying system into a narrower 18-inch footprint — constraints that get harder, not easier, as the chassis shrinks.

The practical takeaway: if raw capacity is the deciding factor, the cheaper 24-inch machine wins outright, in both directions — more place settings and a lower price. The only reason to pay the $849 premium is the narrower chassis, the quieter operation, or the extra features — not because it's "the better dishwasher" generally. Bigger, in this lineup, is also cheaper and roomier, the opposite of what shoppers usually expect.

Installation

Which Kenmore listing fits a renter's kitchen, and which needs a real cutout

None of the three listings in this lineup describes a tank-fed or wheeled machine — the kind of dedicated portable dishwasher that sidesteps a cabinet cutout entirely, the category we cover in our countertop-versus-18-inch comparison. All three Kenmore machines need a hookup and a cabinet opening; the difference is which width of opening, and how plainly each listing says so.

The 18-inch compact's title states "Built-In" outright — unambiguous: a fixed appliance meant to be plumbed into an 18-inch cabinet opening and stay there, with no mention of casters, a finished top panel, or any freestanding configuration. It's built-in or it's nowhere.

The two 24-inch Hybrid Tub listings never use the words "built-in," "freestanding," or "portable" — their titles are entirely about tub construction, noise rating, wash features and capacity. We won't invent an installation claim the listing doesn't make. What the width alone tells us: 24 inches is the standard American built-in dishwasher opening, the cutout size most full-size kitchen dishwashers have occupied for decades. Every one of these three machines needs a cabinet opening, a water line, a drain connection and an electrical hookup — the standard built-in install our depth and cabinet clearance guide walks through in general terms.

So the renter's question isn't "which Kenmore skips the plumbing" — none of them do. It's "which opening does my kitchen already have." A rental with an existing 24-inch dishwasher bay, the far more common cutout size in US kitchens, is a straightforward swap-in for either Hybrid Tub color. A kitchen with only an 18-inch opening is the narrower case the compact fills — worth confirming warranty and installation support before committing, since coverage varies across brands, as our warranty comparison covers. A kitchen with no dishwasher opening at all isn't served by any of these three, regardless of price.

The decision

Which Kenmore model fits which kitchen

Buy a 24-inch Hybrid Tub if

Any one of these is true

You already have a standard 24-inch dishwasher bay. Or you want the largest capacity here — 12 place settings beats the compact's 10. Or budget matters more than noise level or extra wash features. Or the finish choice is the only real decision you want to make. Any one of these settles it in favor of the cheaper, bigger machine.

Buy the 18-inch compact if

All of these are true

Your kitchen has only an 18-inch cabinet opening, or an older slim built-in you're replacing. You specifically want the quieter 45 dBA rating, the EasyFlex 3rd rack, UltraWash Plus and TurboDry. You're prepared to pay roughly $400 more for two fewer place settings. And you've confirmed the plumbing and electrical access a built-in installation requires, since the listing offers no freestanding option.

If neither cutout exists yet

All three machines assume a cabinet opening is either there or can be created — none is the kind of tank-fed or wheeled unit that lets a renter skip plumbing entirely. If your kitchen has no dishwasher opening and modifying cabinetry isn't an option, a dedicated portable model is the better start; our countertop-versus-18-inch comparison and the main buyer's guide cover that class. FAQ

Common questions about the Kenmore lineup

Why does the Kenmore 18-inch compact cost almost twice as much as the 24-inch Hybrid Tub models?

Not capacity — the compact actually holds fewer place settings, 10 against 12. It's what's packed into a narrower chassis: an EasyFlex 3rd rack, an upgraded UltraWash Plus wash tier, a TurboDry drying step, a quieter 45 dBA rating, and a fingerprint-resistant stainless finish, none of which the 24-inch listings include.

What does "Hybrid Tub" mean on Kenmore's 24-inch dishwashers?

The listings don't define it, but "hybrid" generally describes a tub built from more than one material — commonly stainless steel where heat and water exposure is highest, paired with a lighter material elsewhere. Treat that as general industry background, not a confirmed breakdown of this specific model.

Is the black or white Kenmore 24-inch dishwasher the better buy?

Based on published specs, no functional difference — same tub, noise rating, rack, wash modes, 12 place settings, and as of this writing the same $449.99 price. Pick whichever finish matches your kitchen. A price gap, if one appears later, isn't buying you anything the spec sheet can justify.

Does the Kenmore 18-inch compact dishwasher fit a rental kitchen without a cutout?

No. Its listing states "Built-In" directly, with no freestanding or wheeled option, so it needs a genuine 18-inch cabinet opening plus a water line, drain connection and electrical hookup. Neither it nor the two 24-inch models are tank-fed or wheeled; all three need an existing or newly cut cabinet opening first.
