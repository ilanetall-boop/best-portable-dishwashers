The only smart dishwasher here. Is the app worth $150?

Twenty-five portable dishwashers in our database. Twenty-four have a dial, a row of buttons, or both. One — GE Profile's countertop model — talks to a phone through an app called SmartHQ. We looked at what that connection actually does, and what the rest of the price gap is really paying for.

What the app does

What SmartHQ actually adds beyond remote start

GE's SmartHQ is the same app platform the brand uses across its connected ranges, refrigerators and full-size dishwashers, extended to this countertop model through its WiFi radio. On paper, that connection buys three things a dial-and-button machine cannot do: starting or scheduling a cycle from your phone instead of standing at the counter, a push notification when the cycle finishes instead of listening for it from another room, and a record of which cycle you ran and when, useful mainly for troubleshooting if something later seems off.

What it does not do, and this matters more than what it does: it doesn't change the wash itself. The water temperature, the spray pattern, the detergent dose and the six wash cycles are identical whether you press the button on the panel or tap the same cycle name on a screen. SmartHQ is a remote control and a notification system layered onto a machine that already has its own panel — not a different dishwasher underneath. Compare that against the HAVA models, where every control lives on the unit itself: a dial or button array you operate standing in front of it, with no second interface and nothing to pair.

The honest case for the app is narrow and specific: it's for the person who loads the machine before leaving for work and wants it to finish an hour before they're home, or who wants a notification rather than walking over to check a light. If neither of those describes your routine, the app is a feature you paid for and may rarely open — which is a reasonable thing to know before, not after, the purchase.

Without the app

Place settings, programs and build quality, app set aside

Set the WiFi aside entirely and compare the three listings as dishwashers. The GE Profile and the plain HAVA both claim six place settings and six wash programs — on that single line, they're evenly matched. The HAVA with the softener doesn't quote a place-setting figure in the specification we have; its listing leads with the softener and an adjustable rack instead, so take its capacity as unstated rather than assume it matches the other two.

Where the GE Profile pulls ahead on hardware, independent of any screen, is noise and drying. It's rated at 52 dBA, a specific figure neither HAVA listing states — treat both machines' real-world noise as unknown rather than assume silence just because no number is printed. And it finishes with what GE calls Auto Open Dry: the door pops open at the end of the cycle to vent steam out of the tub, which is the most effective drying method available to a countertop machine that has no heating element. Neither HAVA model's listing describes an equivalent mechanism, so if you've been annoyed by damp plastic lids sitting in a closed tub after a cycle, this is a real, app-independent difference.

On certification, the GE Profile carries ENERGY STAR. Our database doesn't show that mark on either HAVA listing, which doesn't mean they fail the standard — many budget countertop units simply skip the certification process — but it does mean GE Profile is the only one of the three you can confirm it on without digging further.

The real fork

A built-in water softener versus WiFi: which upgrade does more for your dishes

Put the two upgrades side by side and they're not even playing the same game. A water softener changes what happens inside the tub during the wash: it conditions the water before it hits the dishes, which is the step that determines whether glasses come out clear or filmed with mineral deposits. WiFi changes nothing about the wash. It changes how you start the cycle and how you learn it's done. One acts on the water; the other acts on your phone.

That makes the choice fall almost entirely on a fact about your house, not about either machine: your local water hardness. If your utility's water-hardness report (most publish one, usually on request or on their website) shows genuinely hard water, the softener in the HAVA at $369.00 is doing something a connected dishwasher cannot do at any price — GE Profile's listing doesn't mention a softener at all, so hard water is a problem the app will never touch. If your water is already soft, the softener does nothing, full stop, and the $17.47 that separates the two HAVA listings is better spent elsewhere, including on the GE Profile's install flexibility.

Our view: check the water-hardness report before comparing either machine to the GE Profile. It's the one piece of information that actually decides which upgrade is wasted on you.

The scenario to plan for

What happens if you never open the app

This is the question that should settle the purchase for most people, because it's the one most roundups skip. If you set up SmartHQ once during install and never open it again, the GE Profile keeps functioning as a normal dishwasher — its physical control panel still starts, stops and selects cycles without the phone anywhere nearby, the same as the HAVA machines. Nothing about the wash depends on the app staying connected, and a WiFi outage doesn't strand a loaded machine.

What you lose is only the layer the app adds: no remote start, no finish notification, no history. In that scenario, you've effectively bought the HAVA with the softener's price tag's neighbor — the GE Profile at $386.47 — for the install flexibility and drying method, while a feature that cost real money sits unused. That's not a defect in the machine; it's a mismatch between the purchase and the routine. The person for whom this trade is worst is someone who's always home when the cycle would run anyway — remote start solves a problem they don't have, and the Auto Open Dry and install flexibility are the only parts of the $386.47 actually earning their keep.

The reverse is also true. Someone who leaves the house for eight hours a day and wants the kitchen to smell like a finished cycle rather than a mid-cycle one when they walk in gets real, daily use out of the exact feature the never-opens-it buyer paid for and ignored. Same machine, same price, opposite verdict — which is why this is a routine question, not a spec-sheet question.

The verdict

Is the premium worth it for a typical two-person kitchen

Buy the GE Profile if

Any one of these is true for you

You're renting and might need to move the dishwasher from a counter into a cabinet or under a sink later. You actually leave the house during the day and want a notification or a remote start, not just the idea of one. A quiet machine in an open-plan kitchen matters to you, and you want a stated 52 dBA figure rather than no number at all. Any one of these justifies the $386.47, with or without the app factored in.

Buy a HAVA model if

Either of these describes you

Your water is measurably hard, in which case the $369.00 softener model solves a real problem the GE Profile cannot touch at any price. Or you simply want six programs and a working tank option for the lowest price in this comparison, in which case the plain $289.00 HAVA does the job with no feature going unused.

For a typical two-person kitchen — moderate soil, no punishing water hardness, a routine where the machine sometimes runs while you're out — our honest read is that the premium is worth it, but not because of the app. It's worth it because install flexibility, a stated noise figure and a real drying mechanism are things a two-person household will use every single cycle, while SmartHQ is something they'll use on the days their schedule happens to call for it. Buy GE Profile for the hardware. Let the app be the feature you discover you like, not the one you paid $97 to get.

FAQ

Common questions about the GE Profile and the app

Does the GE Profile dishwasher need WiFi to run a wash cycle?

No. The physical control panel on the machine starts, stops and selects among its six wash cycles with no app or network connection required. SmartHQ adds remote start, scheduling and finish notifications on top of that panel — it doesn't replace it, and a lost WiFi connection doesn't prevent the dishwasher from washing dishes.

Is the GE Profile actually a better dishwasher than the HAVA models, or just a connected one?

Both, depending on which feature you weigh. Independent of the app, the GE Profile is rated at 52 dBA (a figure neither HAVA listing states), uses an Auto Open Dry mechanism, carries ENERGY STAR certification, and offers three install options — countertop, built-in or under-sink — that the HAVA listings do not. The app is an additional difference on top of those, not the only one.

Does either HAVA model have WiFi?

No. Neither the plain HAVA 6-program countertop model nor the HAVA with the built-in water softener connects to an app in our data. Both are operated entirely through their own control panel, which is also true of every other dishwasher in our broader portable-dishwasher database except the GE Profile.

Is a water softener or WiFi the better $369-versus-$386 upgrade?

It depends on a fact about your house, not about the machines: your water hardness. A built-in softener conditions the wash water itself, which only matters if your water is genuinely hard — check your local utility's water-hardness report. WiFi changes nothing about the wash; it changes how you start the cycle and how you're notified it finished. If your water is already soft, the softener does nothing, and the GE Profile's install flexibility and drying method become the more relevant upgrade.
