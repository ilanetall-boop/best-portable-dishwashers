Buying guide · SPT SD-6513WA, SD-6513W & SD-6513WB

SPT also builds a 24-inch line. Here's what it actually is.

Behind SPT's familiar 18-inch SD-9263 family sits a wider, higher-capacity 24-inch line sold under three listings — SD-6513WB, SD-6513WA and SD-6513W. Two state "10 Place Settings" in the title. One doesn't. The price gap between all three doesn't track what each actually says it is.

SPT's 18-inch SD-9263 family is the listing most roundups default to when a reader needs a real, full-height dishwasher on castors. It isn't the only freestanding line the brand sells. Three listings on Amazon — SD-6513WB, SD-6513WA and SD-6513W — carry a wider, 24-inch-wide cabinet instead, priced from $860.01 to $928.26. That's a genuinely different machine class, not a reskin of the 18-inch platform, and the three listings don't even agree with each other on what they state in their own titles.

This page is only about those three SD-6513 listings: why SPT bothers building a 24-inch portable at all when the 18-inch family already exists, what the WA, W and WB suffixes do and don't change, which one listing quietly skips the "10 Place Settings" claim the other two make front and center, and whether the roughly $73 spread between the cheapest and the most expensive buys anything real. If you already know you want the cheapest of the three, skip ahead to the picks, cheapest first.

Why it exists

Why a 24-inch SPT line exists alongside the 18-inch SD-9263 family

Twenty-four inches is the standard width of a full-size built-in dishwasher slot in an American kitchen. SPT's 18-inch SD-9263 machines are deliberately narrower than that — a size built to roll into general floor space that was never designed around a dishwasher cabinet, at the cost of a smaller rack. The SD-6513 family goes the other way: it matches the width a full-size built-in would occupy, and that extra six inches is exactly what lets SPT fit a 10-place-setting rack into the WB and WA listings, more than the 18-inch line claims.

That trade is the entire reason the 24-inch line exists. A buyer who wants capacity closer to what a full-size built-in holds, but still needs the machine to be freestanding on castors because there's no plumbing or no permission to install one — a rental without a dishwasher cabinet, a kitchen mid-renovation, a house where the built-in died and nobody wants to cut a hole in a different cabinet — is the exact buyer the 18-inch class under-serves and the 24-inch class is built for. It costs more and takes more floor, but it's the closer stand-in for a real built-in.

One brand running two (or more) width classes under a single name isn't unique to SPT. Our breakdown of Kenmore's three very different "portable" dishwashers covers the same pattern at a different brand, and our look at Sunvivi's 18-inch countertop machine against SUNVIVIPRO's 24-inch built-in shows the exact same 18-versus-24-inch size jump showing up again under a closely related name. The lesson repeats: a model number or a brand name doesn't tell you the width class. The listing does.

Model codes

WA, W and WB: what the suffix letters change, and what they don't

SPT doesn't publish a public legend for these suffixes any more for the 24-inch line than it does for its compact countertop series — see our breakdown of the SD-2224 and SD-2225 suffixes for the same caution applied to a different SPT family. The honest approach here is the same: read what each specific listing states, not what the letter is supposed to mean.

Lined up by what their titles actually say:

  • SD-6513WB — "24″ Wide Portable Dishwasher with ENERGY STAR, 6 Wash Programs, 10 Place Settings and Stainless Steel Tub – White." $860.01, the cheapest of the three.
  • SD-6513WA — the identical claim, word for word: ENERGY STAR, 6 Wash Programs, 10 Place Settings, Stainless Steel Tub, White. $866.26, just $6.40 above the WB.
  • SD-6513W — "Energy Star 24″ Portable Stainless Steel Dishwasher – White." No stated wash-program count, no stated place-setting count. $928.26, the most expensive of the three.

So the suffix letters split the family into two groups, not three. WB and WA state exactly the same spec — same certification, same wash-program count, same place-setting count, same tub material, same color — and sit $6.40 apart. Whatever separates them, it isn't anything either title tells you. W is the odd one out: a shorter, older-reading title that drops two of the headline claims the other two make, while costing the most of the three. The letter alone doesn't predict any of that; only the full title does.

What's missing

The one listing that skips "10 Place Settings"

Both the SD-6513WB and the SD-6513WA lead with "10 Place Settings" as a headline claim, right alongside "6 Wash Programs" and the ENERGY STAR badge. The SD-6513W's title stops at "Energy Star 24″ Portable Stainless Steel Dishwasher – White" — no settings figure, no programs count, nothing beyond the certification, the tub material, the width and the color.

That absence doesn't prove the SD-6513W holds fewer place settings or runs fewer programs than its two siblings. It may be the exact same rack behind a shorter, less detailed title — titles get rewritten, trimmed or left untouched across a listing's lifetime for reasons that have nothing to do with the hardware inside. What it does mean is that you can't assume parity from the model number family alone. If capacity and program count matter to your decision — and for a 24-inch machine competing on exactly those two numbers, they usually do — the SD-6513W is the one listing here where you'd want to open the full product page and check the bullet points and dimensions before ordering, rather than taking the "SD-6513" in the name as a guarantee that it matches its siblings.

It's also worth noting what this means for price: the listing that states the least about its capacity is priced the highest of the three. That's backwards from what a shopper skimming three similarly named listings would expect, and it's the clearest reason to read each SD-6513 title on its own rather than treating the family name as interchangeable.

What doesn't change

Error Alarm and Rinse Aid Warning: the two features all three share

Whatever else splits these three listings — place-setting claims, wash-program counts, $73 of price — an Error Alarm and a Rinse Aid Warning are specified on all three. That consistency is worth calling out precisely because it doesn't follow the same WA/WA/W split everything else does: it's a baseline feature of the SD-6513 platform itself, not something that varies listing to listing.

Practically, the Error Alarm is the machine's way of flagging a cycle that can't complete properly — most commonly a water-supply problem at the faucet connection or a door that isn't latched — rather than running a partial cycle and leaving you to discover cold, soapy dishes later. The Rinse Aid Warning does the opposite job: it tells you before the tank runs dry, which matters more on this platform than it would on a simpler machine, because a 24-inch cabinet with a 10-place-setting rack is moving considerably more water and more dishes through each cycle than a compact countertop model, and spotting on glassware from an empty rinse-aid reservoir is one of the most common disappointments across this whole product category.

Because this pair is constant across all three listings, it isn't a reason to prefer one SD-6513 suffix over another — the stated place-setting count, the stated wash-program count and the live price are what actually separate WB, WA and W from each other.

The honest take

Whether the price gap reflects any real difference

Mostly, no. Between the SD-6513WB and the SD-6513WA, $6.40 separates two listings whose titles state the identical spec — same place settings, same programs, same tub, same certification, same color. There's nothing in either title that justifies the gap in either direction; it reads as ordinary listing-to-listing price noise, the same pattern we found between SPT's own SD-2225DWA and SD-2225DW in our breakdown of that compact countertop pair, and the same phenomenon our look at EdgeStar's near-identical countertop listings covers under a different brand. It's a pattern this category produces regularly: the same hardware, listed more than once, priced independently.

Between the WB/WA pair and the SD-6513W, the gap is both larger — $66 to $73 — and pointed the wrong way to call it a premium feature. The SD-6513W states less in its title, not more, yet costs the most of the three. If the SD-6513W's title were simply a trimmed, older version of the same spec, paying more for less information would be a bad trade on principle, even before asking whether the hardware actually matches. Our honest read: start with the cheaper of the WB and WA, whichever is priced lower on the day you're buying, since their stated specs are indistinguishable and you have no reason to pay more for the identical claim. Only consider the SD-6513W if you've opened its current listing, confirmed the place-setting and program counts for yourself, and still prefer it for some other reason — a particular seller, a delivery window, or a feature the shortened title simply didn't repeat.

Whichever SD-6513 listing you land on, it's still a faucet-only, 24-inch freestanding machine, so the connection questions that apply to SPT's 18-inch family apply here too — see the adapters and hose kits that actually fit a portable dishwasher before you assume your existing tap will work out of the box.

FAQ

Common questions about the SD-6513 family

What's the actual difference between the SPT SD-6513WA and SD-6513WB?

Based on what each listing states, nothing: both title claim 10 place settings, 6 wash programs, a stainless steel tub, ENERGY STAR certification and a white finish. The SD-6513WA costs $6.40 more than the SD-6513WB for the identical stated spec, which reads as ordinary listing-to-listing price variation rather than a real upgrade.

Why does the SPT SD-6513W cost more but state less about its specs?

The SD-6513W's title states only its 24-inch width, stainless steel tub, ENERGY STAR certification and white finish — it skips the place-setting and wash-program counts the WB and WA both lead with. At $928.26 it's also priced highest of the three. The shorter title doesn't prove the hardware differs, but it means you can't assume parity; check the full listing before paying the highest price of the three for the least-stated spec.

Do all three SD-6513 models have 10 place settings?

Only two of the three state it. The SD-6513WB and SD-6513WA both state 10 place settings in their titles. The SD-6513W's title states no place-setting figure at all, so that claim can't be confirmed from the title alone.

What do the Error Alarm and Rinse Aid Warning on the SD-6513 family actually do?

Both are specified across all three SD-6513 listings. The Error Alarm flags a cycle that can't complete properly, commonly a water-supply issue at the faucet connection or an unlatched door. The Rinse Aid Warning alerts you before the rinse-aid reservoir runs dry, which matters on a 24-inch, 10-place-setting machine moving more water and more dishes per cycle than a compact countertop model.
